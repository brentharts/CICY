\title{Electrons, Magnetic Fields, and Gauge Holonomy on Toroidal Fibrations of Aperiodic Substrates}
\author{Brent S. Hartshorn \orcidlink{0009-0004-2853-655X}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Alpöge's recent construction of a complex structure on the six-sphere ($S^{6}$) provides a compact complex threefold fibred by tori. We demonstrate that the combinatorial substrate of its cusp fibre precisely matches the Laves substrate of the aperiodic "Hat" monotile at its distinguished proportions. Although full aperiodic tilings do not descend to the torus due to irrational inflation, folding substitution patches reveals that mystic tiles anchor onto exactly six distinct fibre classes corresponding to the oriented double curves of the dual complex. Contrasting this irrational behavior with the integer-inflation three-dimensional Tsiokos's "Chair44" tile, we establish exact geometric defect rates, line defects, and tight-binding spectral convergence across nested torus towers. These results bridge discrete aperiodic geometry with the rigid topological, arithmetic, and lattice constraints governing higher-dimensional manifold constructions. 

\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The cusp fibre $W$ of Alpöge's complex structure on the six-sphere is the
$[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling rolled up on a torus $\TT = \RR^2/\Lam$,
$\Lam = B_0\ZZ^2$, and at the proportions of the Hat that tiling is exactly the
substrate of the aperiodic monotile: hexagon centres are components, edge
midpoints double curves, hexagon vertices triple points. No monotile tiling
descends to $\TT$, since an aperiodic tiling is invariant under no lattice. We
fold a Hat tiling generated by substitution onto $\TT$ anyway. Every tile is a
union of whole kites, so the fold is well defined, and we compute its fibres.
The ``mystic'' tile (the Turtle, ten kites) lands on exactly six points of
the torus at every substitution level from the third on: one per
orientation, each anchored on the midpoint of a double curve of $W$, two
orientations per curve. The six classes form one orbit of the rotation group of
a hexagon, and six is the number of oriented edges of the dual complex of $W$,
which we show is the torus with one vertex, three edges and two triangles. The
count is proved for all levels at once by an exact finite automaton, a
computer-assisted proof, and the Hat itself also has exactly six classes from the
second level on. We show by the boundary statistics of the folded tiling that
equal fibres do not mean equal surroundings. The Hat's inflation is irrational, so it leaves no lattices to descend to.
Tsiokos's Chair44, a three-dimensional einstein tile, inflates by the integer $2$, and
its supertile lattices $2^M\ZZ^3$ give a tower of nested tori $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$. For this
tower we prove that a patch made of whole level-$M$ blocks agrees with its translate by
$2^Mv$ on at least a fraction $1-c(M)/8^M$ of its cells, where
$c(M)=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst$ counts the addresses at which the substitution's
modular coincidence has not yet occurred (a finite recurrence certificate), and we measure
the agreement on a patch of $\SSChPatchCells$ cells: $\SSChAgreeThree$, $\SSChAgreeFour$ and
$\SSChAgreeFive$ at $M=3,4,5$. Scanned over all translates by the same FFT at matched
size, the Hat has near-periods too but weaker ones (best agreement $0.975$ against
$0.998$, and none at $0.99$ against fifty). The undetermined addresses are exactly four tubes along the body
diagonals, a line defect one dimension below the area-law interface of the supertiles. The frame mismatch under the
translation, valued in the rotation group $O$ of the cube, is supported on them with dihedral values (and all of $O$ at the sixteen end and junction cells), but
it is not a function on the torus, so it carries no flat connection and no class in $H^1(T_M;O)$. On the periodic approximants
$\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$ the integrated density of states of a tight-binding model, and every Bragg amplitude at a dyadic wave
vector, converge at the same geometric rate $4^{-M}$, with an explicit bound. The contrast with the Hat is one of kind and not an identity:
the six-sphere contains no Chair44, and two tempting matches with it, the $24$ simplices
round a vertex of the Kuhn triangulation of $\ZZ^3$ and the $(3,4,\infty)$ group against
the rotation group of the cube, are shown to be coincidences.
The paper also records why the lattice data
behind the construction is forced, that its monodromy is achiral and its
lattice not self-dual over $\ZZ$, and three exact contacts with standard
objects (a polytope volume, a Kodaira configuration, and central charges).
Read as a gauge theory without principal bundles, the torus fibre carries the structure of a symplectic lattice with
a marked vector; its automorphism group is the integral Jacobi group $\SL_2(\ZZ)\ltimes\mathrm{Heis}$, and the
holonomy is the kernel of a character onto $\ZZ/12$, so it has index $\SSGaIndex$ and is Zariski dense in
the real Jacobi group; that character is the multiplier of $e(\beta/12)\,\theta_1(\tau,\mu)/\eta(\tau)$, so the holonomy is the
invariance group of one explicit function on the period laws. A tight-binding electron on the cusp fibre's substrate has Bloch bands that are
functions of the roots of $A_2$ with nodal lines on the Weyl mirrors, a magnetic field that restricts to the Hat as the
restriction of a connection (with flux period $1$ for the substrate and $\tfrac12$ for the Hat), and Chern numbers
that two independent routes reproduce.

In 1948 Hopf asked whether the six-sphere admits a complex
structure~\cite{Hopf48}. In August 2026 Alpöge posted a construction
answering yes~\cite{Alpoge}: a compact complex threefold $X$, fibred over
$\mathbb{P}^1$ by complex 2-tori, with a cusp fibre $W$ and two multiple
fibres over three points, proved diffeomorphic to $S^6$. Engel gave a
pedagogical account~\cite{Engel}, and Alexeev formalised the headline
statement in Lean~4~\cite{Alexeev}. The construction contradicts a published
corollary of Campana, Demailly and Peternell~\cite{CDP20}, and Alpöge locates
where the two accounts part. At the time of writing the result is not
refereed.

Nearly all of the proof is analysis and topology. It stands, however, on two
$4\times4$ integer matrices $T_1,T_2$ of orders $3$ and $4$ whose product is
unipotent, and much of what is proved is decided by linear algebra over $\ZZ$.
We work only with that finite layer, exactly, and ask what it is connected to.
The answer we pursue is the aperiodic monotile.

\paragraph{The substrate.} The cusp fibre $W$ is a degree-six del Pezzo surface
glued to itself, and its strata have the incidence structure of the $A_2$
triangulation of the plane modulo $\Lam$. The substrate of the Hat, the kite
graph of Smith, Myers, Kaplan and Goodman-Strauss~\cite{SmithHat}, is at the
Hat's own proportions the $[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling, and \cref{thm:laves} shows
that tiling is the Hasse diagram of that triangulation (\cref{fig:dictionary}).
The cusp fibre is the substrate \emph{rolled up}.

\paragraph{Folding a tiling.} A periodic tiling can be rolled up and an
aperiodic one cannot, so the substrate descends and the tiling does not. That
concerns whole tilings. A finite patch of a Hat tiling is a union of tiles, each
a union of whole kites, and the quotient $\pi:\RR^2\to\TT$ sends it to a finite
multiset on the torus. \Cref{sec:rollup} does this for a substitution patch and
records, for each of the twelve bond orbits of $\TT$, how often the bond is a
tile boundary (\cref{fig:rollup}); none is always or never, which is the
certificate of non-descent.

(b) The torus, each bond orbit weighted by how often that bond is a tile

\paragraph{Mystic fibres.} We follow the mystic, the ten-kite tile, through the
fold. Two mystics are in the same \emph{fibre class} if they differ by an
element of $\Lam$. In the three-step patch (443 tiles, 51 mystic) the mystics
form exactly six fibre classes, the same six occur at four steps, and five of
them at two steps. The classes are the six orientations of the mystic; in each,
a distinguished vertex lies over the midpoint of one of the three double curves
$D_a,D_b,D_c$, and orientations differing by $180^\circ$ share a double curve
(\cref{fig:n3}). The six classes are one orbit of the rotation group of a
hexagon, and six is the number of oriented edges of the dual complex of $W$
(\cref{sec:dual,sec:mystic}). The same six occur at every level from the third
on, which \cref{thm:six} proves by an exact finite automaton (a computer-assisted
proof, and the Hat has six classes as well); the dependence of the surroundings on the
class is observed, not proved.

\paragraph{An integer inflation.} The Hat does not descend because its inflation factor is
irrational and its tiling is aperiodic, so no lattice is left invariant. Tsiokos's
Chair44~\cite{Tsiokos1}, a three-dimensional einstein tile, is aperiodic with the
\emph{integer} inflation $2$, so its level-$M$ supertiles sit on the nested lattices
$2^M\ZZ^3$ and the tiling is exhausted by the tori $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$. \Cref{sec:chair44}
proves that a patch of whole level-$M$ blocks agrees with its translate by $2^Mv$ on at
least $1-c(M)/8^M$ of its cells, with $c(M)=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst$, measures it on a
patch of $\SSChPatchCells$ cells (agreement $\SSChAgreeThree$, $\SSChAgreeFour$,
$\SSChAgreeFive$ at $M=3,4,5$, against the Hat's best $0.796$ on a different footing), and
settles two tempting matches with the six-sphere as coincidences. It is a contrast of kind:
the six-sphere contains no Chair44.

\paragraph{A gauge group from the fibre.} \Cref{sec:gauge} runs Gomes's recipe \cite{Gomes} on the lattice itself: the
structure every torus fibre carries is a symplectic lattice with a marked vector, its automorphisms are the integral
Jacobi group, and the holonomy is the kernel of a character onto $\ZZ/12$ (\cref{thm:gauge-index}). The four near-miss
classes of \cref{sec:forced} then become the four divisibilities of the marked vector.

\paragraph{A theta function whose multiplier is the holonomy.} \Cref{sec:theta} proves from the classical transformation laws that the character
of \cref{thm:gauge-index} is the multiplier of $e(\beta/12)\theta_1/\eta$, so the monodromy group is the exact invariance group of that function.

\paragraph{Electrons on the substrate.} \Cref{sec:hofstadter} puts a tight-binding electron on the graph that the cusp fibre
is rolled up from. Its bands are functions of the $A_2$ roots, a magnetic field is a connection that restricts to the Hat with no gauge
chosen on the tiling, and the Chern numbers of the gaps are computed by two routes.

\paragraph{Curves on surfaces.} \Cref{sec:surface} reads the dual complex of \cref{sec:dual} as an ideal triangulation of the
once-punctured torus, the setting of the curve formalism of Arkani-Hamed et al.~\cite{AHFPT} and of the leading singularities of
Carr\^olo and Figueiredo~\cite{CF}. As a fatgraph it is a two-loop genus-one vacuum diagram with no external state, so it carries no amplitude. Its quiver is the Markov quiver, the monodromy blocks of $T_1,T_2$ generate $\SL_2(\ZZ)$, and the Lucas
recurrence of the $n$-gon leading singularity is the trace of the $n$-th power of a $2\times2$ matrix, whose values at the
$(3,4,\infty)$ data are the orders $3$, $4$ and the unipotent. Two matches are shown to be contrasts, not links.

\section{The cusp fibre is the monotile's substrate}
\label{sec:laves}

\subsection{The substrate at the Hat point}

The Hat of~\cite{SmithHat,SmithSpectre} is a union of eight kites
(\cref{fig:hat}). A kite has legs $a$ from a hexagon centre to an edge midpoint
and $b$ from that edge midpoint to a hexagon vertex, meeting at right angles at
the edge midpoint, along directions that are multiples of $30^\circ$;
coordinates lie in $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ and are exact. Around a hexagon centre the six
edge midpoints are $a\,e_k$, $e_k = u_{30^\circ+60^\circ k}$, and each carries
two outer kite corners $a\,e_k \pm b\,e_k^{\perp}$, twelve per hexagon. Adjacent
corners coincide exactly when
\begin{equation}
  \overbrace{a\,e_k + b\,e_k^{\perp}}^{\text{corner of kite }k}
  \;=\;
  \overbrace{a\,e_{k+1} - b\,e_{k+1}^{\perp}}^{\text{corner of kite }k+1},
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  a=\tfrac a2+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}b
  \ \text{ and }\
  b = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}a-\tfrac b2
  \ \Longleftrightarrow\ a=\sqrt3\,b .
  \label{eq:merge}
\end{equation}
So at the Hat's proportions $a:b=\sqrt3:1$, and at no others, the twelve corners
merge in pairs into the six vertices of the $[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling. With
$a=\sqrt3$, $b=1$ the kite has area $\underbrace{2}_{\text{two triangles}}
\cdot\tfrac12 ab=\sqrt3$, and the Hat, eight kites, has area $8\sqrt3$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{ss_hat_kites.pdf}
\caption{The Hat at $a:b=\sqrt3:1$ as eight whole kites of the $[3.4.6.4]$ Laves
lattice. Circles are hexagon centres (valence 6), squares edge midpoints
(valence 4), triangles hexagon vertices (valence 3). The thirteen-sided outline
has area $8\sqrt3$ in units where $b=1$.}
\label{fig:hat}
\end{figure}

On the six-sphere's side, the cusp fibre is built from a toric fan whose
cross-section is the $A_2$ triangulation of the plane~\cite{Alpoge}: its
vertices give the components of $W$, its edges the double curves, its triangles
the triple points, and the quotient by $B_0\ZZ^2$ identifies them.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:laves}
At the Hat point $a:b=\sqrt3:1$ the monotile substrate is the Hasse diagram of
the $A_2$ triangulation: its sites are the cells (hexagon centres the vertices,
edge midpoints the edges, hexagon vertices the triangles) and its bonds are
exactly the cover relations vertex $<$ edge $<$ triangle. At no other
proportions is it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Computation]
Embed $A_2$ by $e_1\mapsto 2a\,u_{30^\circ}$, $e_2\mapsto 2a\,u_{90^\circ}$ with
vertices at hexagon centres; edge midpoints and triangle centroids follow
(\cref{fig:dictionary}). For the two-ring patch at the Hat point all
\SSLavesSites{} sites coincide exactly, in $\QQ(\sqrt3)$, with cells of the
matching kind, and the \SSLavesIncidences{} cover relations among them are
exactly its \SSLavesBonds{} bonds. That the corners merge at no other ratio is
\eqref{eq:merge}. At the Spectre's proportions $a=b$, and at $17/10:1$ (within
$2\%$ of $\sqrt3$), the corners stay apart: the patch has \SSSpectreSites{} and
\SSNearHatSites{} sites, its ``vertex'' sites have valence
\SSSpectreVertexValence{} rather than $3$, and the graph is a different graph,
not a metric distortion of the same one. The substrate is periodic and the patch
contains a fundamental domain with every cell incident to it, so agreement on
the patch is agreement everywhere.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The dictionary}

Since $|\det B_0|=1$, translation by $B_0\ZZ^2$ is exactly translation of the
Laves tiling by its period lattice, so the cusp fibre is that tiling rolled up
on $\RR^2/B_0\ZZ^2$ (\cref{fig:torus,fig:dp6}):

  +\underbrace{2}_{T}\bigr)}_{\text{6 sites}}
  -\underbrace{\bigl(\underbrace{6}_{\text{centre--edge}}+
  \underbrace{6}_{\text{edge--vertex}}\bigr)}_{\text{12 bonds}}
  +\underbrace{6}_{\text{kites}} = 0 .
  \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}

\end{equation}
each hexagon side is a $(-1)$-curve and each edge midpoint is bonded to two
hexagon vertices.

\subsection{What descends, and what does not}

\Cref{thm:laves} is an identity of substrates, not of tilings. The Laves tiling
is periodic and its quotient by $B_0\ZZ^2$ is a compact object, the cusp fibre.
A Hat tiling is aperiodic: it is invariant under no translation lattice,
$B_0\ZZ^2$ included, so it does not descend to $W$ or any torus. Both inherit
their lattice from $A_2$, and the six-sphere knows nothing about monotiles; what
they share is that lattice and, by \cref{thm:laves}, the single point of the
monotile family at which the substrate \emph{is} its incidence structure.
\Cref{sec:rollup} tests non-descent on a computed tiling and \cref{sec:mystic}
asks what survives when the tiling is folded onto the torus anyway.

\section{The dual complex of the cusp fibre}
\label{sec:dual}

The dual complex of a normal-crossing fibre has a vertex for each component, an
edge for each double curve and a triangle for each triple point. For $W$ the
incidences are those of the $A_2$ triangulation modulo $\Lam$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:dualcx}
The dual complex $\Delta(W)$ has $1$ vertex $E_0$, $3$ edges $D_a,D_b,D_c$ (along
$e_1$, $e_2-e_1$, $e_2$) and $2$ triangles $P$ (lower) and $Q$ (upper); every
edge borders both triangles. It is the torus, with
\begin{equation}
  \partial P = \underbrace{D_a + D_b - D_c}_{\partial_2 P}, \qquad
  \partial Q = \underbrace{D_c - D_a - D_b}_{\partial_2 Q} = -\partial P,
  \qquad \partial_1 = 0,
  \label{eq:boundary}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\underbrace{H_0=\ZZ}_{\operatorname{rk}1},\quad
  \underbrace{H_1=\ZZ^3/\langle(1,1,-1)\rangle\cong\ZZ^2}_{\operatorname{rk}2},\quad
  \underbrace{H_2=\ker\partial_2=\ZZ\langle P+Q\rangle}_{\operatorname{rk}1},\\
  &\chi=\overbrace{1-2+1}^{\text{Betti}}=\overbrace{1-3+2}^{V-E+F}=0,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:homology}
\end{equation}
and $\pi_1\Delta(W)=\langle a,b,c\mid ab=c,\ ba=c\rangle=\ZZ^2\cong\Lam$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The vertices of the $A_2$ triangulation form $\Lam$ itself ($|\det B_0|=1$), so
there is one vertex per domain. Its edges fall into three $\Lam$-orbits of
directions, and its triangles into the two orbits ``lower'' $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$
and ``upper'' $(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)$ of the unit parallelogram cut along its short
diagonal. Reading the boundary of $P$ round its vertices gives $e_1$, then
$e_2-e_1$, then $-e_2$, which is \eqref{eq:boundary}; the boundary of $Q$ is
the negative. Each edge is a loop, so $\partial_1=0$. The matrix of $\partial_2$
has the single primitive column $(1,1,-1)^T$ up to sign, giving
\eqref{eq:homology}. The fundamental group is generated by the three loops with
one relation per triangle, $ab=c$ from $P$ and $ba=c$ from $Q$, hence $ab=ba$.
The counts and incidences are asserted against \texttt{sixsphere.cusp\_fibre()}
by the figure script before anything is drawn.
\end{proof}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{ss_dual_complex.pdf}
\caption{The dual complex of $W$. (a) The $A_2$ triangulation modulo
$B_0\ZZ^2$: triangles tinted by class $P$ or $Q$, double curves as orange lines
in three styles (solid $D_a$, dashed $D_b$, dotted $D_c$), the domain as the

\section{Rolling up a Hat tiling}
\label{sec:rollup}

\paragraph{The patch.} Tilings are generated by the substitution system of
\texttt{spectre.py}, which builds $\mathrm{Tile}(a,b)$ for any proportions; at
$(1,\sqrt3)$ it is the Hat, with the mystic $\Gamma_2$ at $(\sqrt3,1)$. The
script asserts that every tile vertex lies on a site of the $[3.4.6.4]$ lattice
at $a:b=\sqrt3:1$, every Hat is eight whole kites and every mystic ten. A patch
after $n$ steps is a disc: $443$ tiles for $n=3$ and $656$ for $n=4$.

\paragraph{The certificate.} Call a lattice bond $b$ a \emph{tile boundary},
$\beta(b)=1$, if its two adjacent kites lie in different tiles, and $\beta(b)=0$
otherwise. For a bond orbit $o$ of $\TT$ (six centre--edge and six edge--vertex
orbits) put
\begin{equation}
  f(o)=\frac{\overbrace{\#\{b\in o:\ \beta(b)=1\}}^{\text{boundary bonds in the patch}}}
  {\underbrace{\#\{b\in o\}}_{N_o}} .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:descent}
If a tiling is invariant under $\Lam$, then $\beta(b+\lambda)=\beta(b)$ for all
$\lambda\in\Lam$, so $\beta$ is constant on each orbit and $f(o)\in\{0,1\}$ for
all twelve orbits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A tile boundary is determined by the tiling, and $\lambda$ maps the tiling to
itself, hence maps boundary bonds to boundary bonds and the others to the others.
Orbits of $\TT$ are the $\Lam$-orbits of bonds.
\end{proof}

For the patches, every $f(o)$ lies strictly inside $(0,1)$: from $0.338$ to
$0.470$ for $n=3$ and from $0.323$ to $0.480$ for $n=4$
(\cref{fig:rollup}(b,c)); the script asserts that none is $0$ or $1$. Their mean
is
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\tfrac1{12}\textstyle\sum_o f(o)}_{0.403}\;\approx\;
  \underbrace{\frac{\#\{\text{boundary bonds}\}}{\#\{\text{bonds}\}}}_{0.402}
  \qquad(n=4),
\end{equation}
the two differing only because the orbit sizes $N_o$ differ by a few bonds.

\paragraph{Overlap with translates.} For $t=iE_1+jE_2\ne0$ let
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(t)=\frac{\#\{b:\ b,\,b+t\in\text{patch},\ \beta(b)=\beta(b+t)\}}
  {\underbrace{\#\{b:\ b,\,b+t\in\text{patch}\}}_{>0}} ,
\end{equation}
which would be $1$ for every $t\in\Lam$ if the tiling were invariant. For $n=4$
the best non-identity agreement is $\alpha=0.796$, at $(i,j)=(-3,-5)$
(\cref{fig:overlap}), and no translate gives $1$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{ss_hat_overlap.pdf}
\caption{The agreement $\alpha(t)$ of the four-step patch's boundary pattern with its own lattice translates $iE_{1} + jE_{2}$. The identity shift is trivially $1.00$, while the optimal non-identity translation occurs at $t = -3E_{1} - 5E_{2}$ with an overlap score of $0.796$. A tiling invariant under $B_0\mathbb{Z}^2$ would exhibit $1.00$ at every lattice point; the absence of any other unit score serves as a finite-patch certificate of non-descent, with partial peaks ($\sim 80\%$) capturing local self-similar cluster alignments.}
\label{fig:overlap}
\end{figure}

These are finite-patch estimates: the fractions depend on the patch radius, and
the statement they illustrate, that a Hat tiling is invariant under no
translation, is the theorem of~\cite{SmithHat} and not something they prove.

\section{Monotile Mystic fibres}
\label{sec:mystic}

\paragraph{Set-up.} Let $E_1=(3,\sqrt3)$, $E_2=(0,2\sqrt3)$, $\Lam=\ZZ E_1+\ZZ E_2$
and $\pi:\RR^2\to\TT=\RR^2/\Lam$. Around each hexagon centre $c\in\Lam$ there are
six kites with vertices $c,\ c+\sqrt3\,u_{30+60k},\ c+2\,u_{60k+60},\
c+\sqrt3\,u_{90+60k}$. Areas fit:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{|\det(E_1,E_2)|}_{6\sqrt3}=\underbrace{6}_{\text{kites per domain}}
  \times\underbrace{\sqrt3}_{\text{kite}},
\end{equation}
so $\pi$ identifies a kite of the plane with one of the six kites of $\TT$
according to its index $\bmod\,6$. Corner angles are $60^\circ$ (centre),
$90^\circ$ (edge midpoint, twice) and $120^\circ$ (hexagon vertex).

A \emph{mystic} is a copy $gM_0+x$ of a fixed ten-kite polygon $M_0$ with a
distinguished vertex $v_0$ (the first in the vertex list), $g$ a rotation and $x$
a site.

\begin{definition}
Two mystics $M,M'$ of a patch are in the same \emph{fibre class} if $M'=M+\lambda$
for some $\lambda\in\Lam$. The fibre point of $M$ is $\pi$ of its centroid.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:six}
Six kites meet at a site only at a hexagon centre; four meet at an edge midpoint
and three at a hexagon vertex:
$\underbrace{360^\circ/60^\circ}_{6}$, $\underbrace{360^\circ/90^\circ}_{4}$,
$\underbrace{360^\circ/120^\circ}_{3}$. Hence a translation preserving the kite
tiling lies in $\Lam$, and the rotation $\rho$ by $60^\circ$ about a hexagon
centre preserves both the kite tiling and $\Lam$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A translation preserving the tiling preserves the number of kites at each site,
so it maps centres to centres, which form the single coset $\Lam$ (one per
domain). For $\rho$: $\rho(E_1)=E_2$ and $\rho(E_2)=E_2-E_1$, both in $\Lam$, and
the kite tiling is invariant under the $6$-fold rotation about any centre.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:bound}
In a tiling by whole kites whose mystics are rotations and translations of one
polygon $M_0$ with anchor $v_0$, the mystics fall into at most
$\underbrace{12}_{\text{first-edge angles}}\times\underbrace{6}_{\text{sites mod }\Lam}=72$
fibre classes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every tile edge is a union of kite edges, whose directions lie in $30^\circ\ZZ$,
so the first edge of $gM_0$ has at most $12$ angles. The anchor is a site, and
mod $\Lam$ there are $1+3+2=6$ sites. Two mystics with equal angle and equal
anchor mod $\Lam$ differ by an element of $\Lam$.
\end{proof}

\paragraph{Why six, structurally.} The six classes are not an arbitrary list.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:orbit}
The observed classes are one orbit of the rotation $\rho$ of \cref{lem:six} acting
on $(\theta,\text{anchor})$, and $\rho$ acts freely:
\begin{equation}
  \#\text{orbit}=\frac{\overbrace{|\langle\rho\rangle|}^{6}}
  {\underbrace{|\operatorname{Stab}(M_0)|}_{1}}=6 .
\end{equation}
Under $k\mapsto\rho^k$ the anchor passes through the edge classes in the cyclic
order $D_a\to D_c\to D_b\to D_a\to\cdots$, so by the Chinese remainder theorem
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\ZZ/6}_{\text{classes}}\;\cong\;
  \underbrace{\ZZ/3}_{k\bmod3:\ \text{double curve}}\times
  \underbrace{\ZZ/2}_{k\bmod2:\ \text{orientation of the edge}} ,
\end{equation}
that is, (double curve) $\times$ (sign) $=3\times2=2E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\rho$ preserves the kite tiling and $\Lam$ (\cref{lem:six}), so it maps mystic
placements to mystic placements, raising the first-edge angle by $60^\circ$. The
stabiliser is trivial because $M_0$ has no rotational symmetry (any nontrivial
rotation changes its edge-angle sequence). The edge midpoints of the hexagon at
the origin are $\sqrt3\,u_{30^\circ+60^\circ j}$, which mod $\Lam$ are $E_1/2$,
$E_2/2$ and $(E_2-E_1)/2\equiv(E_1+E_2)/2$ for $j=0,1,2$ and repeat with period
$3$ because $-E_1/2\equiv E_1/2$. These are $D_a,D_c,D_b$. That the observed
classes are exactly this orbit is the content of \cref{obs:orient}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[six classes at every level; computer-assisted]
\label{thm:six}
For every $n\ge3$, and for every one of the nine root labels, the mystics of the full
level-$n$ patch fall into exactly the six classes of \cref{obs:orient}, one for each
oriented double curve of $\Delta(W)$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}[Proof (by an exact finite automaton)]
In the substitution of~\cite{SmithHat}, as implemented in the code of
\url{https://github.com/brentharts/spectre}, the eight child placements at level $m$
are rigid motions $\Theta_k=(R\,\mathrm{Rot}_k,\ R\,t_k)$ with $R$ the mirror
$x\mapsto-x$. The rotation parts $\mathrm{Rot}_k$, multiples of $60^\circ$, are the same
at every level; only the translations $t_k$ change, and they are fixed by the four-point
quad $Q^{m-1}$ of the level below. The rotation $\rho$ by $60^\circ$ and the mirror
preserve $\Lam$ (\cref{lem:six}); in the basis $(E_1,E_2)$ they are
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&-1\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ and
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&0\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. So each formula is
well defined on $\TT$, and the pair $(Q^m\bmod\Lam,\ W_m)$, where $W_m(X)$ is the set of
placements $(g,c)$ of the mystic inside a level-$m$ supertile of label $X$,
evolves by an exact deterministic map on a finite set, with rational arithmetic
throughout (the base quad lies in $\tfrac16\ZZ^2$). Run until the first repeat: the state
at level $5$ equals the state at level $3$, so the sequence has period $2$ from
level $3$ on, and one pass settles every $n$. At levels $3,4,5$ the set $W_m$ has $6$
placements. The class of a placement is (reflection, first-edge angle, anchor
$-$ registration offset, mod $\Lam$); the registration offset of the whole level-$n$
patch is the same for every placement, which is the assertion that every tile vertex is
a site, and the anchor is the midpoint of $D_a$, $D_c$ or $D_b$. The six classes are
$30^\circ a,\,90^\circ c,\,150^\circ b,\,210^\circ a,\,270^\circ c,\,330^\circ b$, a set
invariant under $\rho$ and the mirror.
\end{proof}
\begin{observation}[the Hat]
\label{obs:hatsix}
The same automaton, run for the Hat (every tile but the mystic), gives exactly six
classes of (reflection, first-edge angle, anchor site) for each $n=2,\dots,10$, with
three anchor sites and six angles, and five classes at $n=1$; direct counting on whole
patches agrees for $n\le6$ (\cref{tab:whole}).
We have stated the proof for the mystic only; for the Hat the count is a computation to
$n=10$.
\end{observation}
The proof is computer-assisted and rests on the encoding of the substitution in the code
it uses: the claim is exactly that the automaton built from that code has the stated
states. It was checked against the geometry of whole patches (\cref{tab:whole}, to
$n=7$, $2.1$ million tiles) and for each of the nine root labels at levels $3$ to $6$,
and the class sizes of the full patch can be read off to $n=12$ (about $7.3\times10^9$
mystics). The bound $72$ of \cref{prop:bound} is far from sharp: only
$6/72=1/12$ of the possible classes occur.

\paragraph{Same fibre, different surroundings.} Equal fibre points do not
contradict non-descent. For each mystic in the inner $60\%$ of the patch record
the multiset of (label, angle) of the tiles it touches. For $n=3$ three of the six
classes contain two environments among their interior members (the rest one),
and for $n=4$ again three classes show two, with one to nine interior mystics per
class. The counts are small and are an indication only. The logic is the safe
part: a tiling that descends has identical surroundings throughout a class
(\cref{prop:descent}), so a class with two environments is an obstruction, the
tile-level form of the boundary-fraction certificate.

\paragraph{Folding one mystic.} A mystic has ten kites and $\TT$ six, so it covers

times. The mystic of $30^\circ$ in the three-step patch folds onto the six torus
kites with multiplicities $m=(2,2,1,2,2,1)$, $\sum_k m_k=10$
(\cref{fig:n3}(a)); other orientations give $\rho$-rotations of this pattern.

\section{The Flag Class: Block Structure of the Monodromy Matrices}
\label{sec:flag}

Write $V = \ZZ^4$ with basis $(\gamma, u, w, \delta)$. Alpöge's
matrices fix $\gamma$, preserve $\langle \gamma, u, w\rangle$, and act
trivially on the top quotient (\cite[Lemma~2.7]{Alpoge}); so each has the
block form
\begin{equation}
  T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & r & s \\ 0 & M & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad M \in \SL_2(\ZZ),\ r \in \ZZ^{1\times 2},\ c \in \ZZ^{2\times 1},
  \label{eq:flag}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the monodromy of the elliptic curve with period $\tau$ ---
modulo $\gamma$, the first period vector is $\tau u + w$ --- and hence a
Kodaira monodromy \cite{graf2020}. We call pairs $(T_1, T_2)$ of this shape the \emph{flag
class}. Everything in \cref{sec:triangle,sec:forced} is a statement about
the flag class; \cref{prop:flagforced} says that this is less of a restriction
than it looks.

\begin{proposition}[the flag shape is forced]
\label{prop:flagforced}
Let $T_1,T_2\in\GL_4(\ZZ)$ with $T_1^3=T_2^4=I$ and $T_0=(T_1T_2)^{-1}=I+N$, where
$N^2=0$ and $\operatorname{rank}N=2$, and suppose the coinvariants $V_G$ of
$G=\langle T_1,T_2\rangle$ are $\ZZ$. Then there is a basis of $V$ in which both have
the shape \eqref{eq:flag}, with $M_1,M_2\in\SL_2(\ZZ)$ of orders $3$ and $4$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$W=\operatorname{im}(T_1-I)+\operatorname{im}(T_2-I)$ is the kernel of $V\to V_G$, so it is a
saturated invariant sublattice of rank three with trivial action on $V/W\cong\ZZ$.
\emph{No split.} A fixed vector $\gamma\notin W_\QQ$ would give
$V_\QQ=W_\QQ\oplus\QQ\gamma$ with $N\gamma=0$, so $N$ would be a square-zero map on a
three-dimensional space, of rank at most $1$, not $2$.
\emph{Constituents.} Every constituent of $V_\QQ$ is a representation of
$\mathbb Z/3*\mathbb Z/4$ on which $T_0$ is unipotent. A character has
$\omega^ai^b=1$, so it is trivial. If all constituents were trivial, each $T_j$ would be a
finite-order unipotent and so $T_j=I$ and $N=0$. A non-trivial two-dimensional
constituent has $T_1$ of characteristic polynomial $\Phi_3$ (if $T_1=I$ on it then
$T_0=T_2^{-1}$ has finite order and is unipotent, so is trivial), and $T_2$ of
characteristic polynomial $\Phi_4$: determinants must multiply to $1$, which rules out
$\Phi_1\Phi_2$, and $T_2=-I$ gives $-T_1$ for $T_0^{-1}$, of trace $1$, not unipotent.
So $V_\QQ$ has constituents $M$ (this one), and two trivial ones, one of which is
$V/W$.
\emph{Order.} If $M$ were a sub of $W$ with trivial quotient, $G$ would act trivially on
the rank-two lattice $V/M$ (again, finite-order unipotent), so $V_G$ would have rank at
least $2$. So $W^G$ has rank one, with a primitive generator $\gamma$, and $W/\ZZ\gamma$ is a
free rank-two lattice on which $G$ acts through $M_1,M_2$. Lifting a basis of
$W/\ZZ\gamma$ and a generator of $V/W$ gives \eqref{eq:flag}.
\end{proof}
The middle block remains to be checked: the product of an order-three and an
order-four element of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ is unipotent of type $\mathrm I_1$ in every case
found ($20$ pairs in a box of $4$, $52$ in a box of $7$), which is observed and not
proved, and is the assumption behind the choice of $M_1$ in \cref{sec:forced}. The
argument is on paper and has not been formalised.

The quantities we need are the paper's. The cusp monodromy is
$T_0 = (T_1T_2)^{-1} = I + N$; the fibre over the cusp is a single copy of
the degree-six del Pezzo surface exactly when $|\det B_0| = 1$, where $B_0$
is induced by $M_0 - I$ on the dual lattice \cite[Lemma~2.6]{Alpoge}; and for twists $\ell_0, \ell_1, \ell_2$ the
presentation of \cite[Theorem~7.17]{Alpoge}, abelianised, gives
\begin{equation}
  |H_1(X)| = |p|, \qquad p = \underbrace{m_1m_2\,\ell_0}_{m_1m_2\ \mid} - \underbrace{m_2\,\ell_1}_{m_2\ \mid} - \underbrace{m_1\,\ell_2}_{m_1\ \mid},
  \label{eq:p}
\end{equation}
with $(m_1, m_2) = (3, 4)$ for the paper. A twist is admissible when the
logarithmic transform it defines is free, which for the paper's data is
$3 \nmid \ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ odd, a rule that follows from a fixed-point computation on the real torus.

\section{Results: Why (3, 4, \texorpdfstring{$\infty$}{infinity}) Is the Only Allowed Triangle Group}
\label{sec:triangle}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:triangle}
In the flag class, a pair $(T_1, T_2)$ of orders $(m_1, m_2)$, with
$(m_1, m_2, \infty)$ hyperbolic, a unipotent cusp, and $|p| = 1$ for some
twist, has $\{m_1, m_2\} = \{3, 4\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The middle block $M_j$ has the same finite order as $T_j$ (a block-unipotent
matrix of finite order is the identity), and the finite orders in
$\SL_2(\ZZ)$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6$. The only element of order two is $-I$,
which is central; if $M_1 = -I$ then $M_0 = (M_1M_2)^{-1} = -M_2^{-1}$ has
trace $-\operatorname{tr} M_2 \in \{1, 0, -1\}$ for $m_2 \in \{3, 4, 6\}$,
so the cusp is not unipotent. This removes every $(2, m)$. Finally, by \eqref{eq:p} each term of $p$ is divisible by $\gcd(m_1,m_2)$, so
\[
  \underbrace{\gcd(m_1,m_2)}_{d}\;\Big|\;p
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |p|=1\ \text{requires}\ \underbrace{d=1}_{\text{coprime orders}};
\] among the hyperbolic pairs from
$\{3, 4, 6\}$ only $(3,4)$ is coprime.
\end{proof}

The gcd alone therefore disposes of $(3,3)$, $(4,4)$ and $(6,6)$. The
survey finds that they fail earlier too, for a reason of independent
interest:

\begin{sloppypar}
The congruence is short enough to machine-check for every twist: the Lean
facts \texttt{gcd\_obstruction\_3\_6} and \texttt{gcd\_obstruction\_4\_6}
state $3 \mid 18\ell_0 - 6\ell_1 - 3\ell_2$ and $2 \mid 24\ell_0 - 6\ell_1 -
4\ell_2$ for all integers, by \texttt{omega}.
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{observation}
\label{obs:sign}
Enumerating $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ elements in a box, $(4,4)$ has no pair whose
product is $\pm$(unipotent), and $(3,3)$ and $(6,6)$ have parabolic
products only as $-$(unipotent): an $\mathrm{I}_2^*$ cusp, never
$\mathrm{I}_n$. The outcome is the same in the boxes $\pm 4$ and $\pm 7$.
For $(3,3)$ the mechanism is visible in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\ZZ)
= \langle s\rangle * \langle t\rangle$, where $t \cdot sts^{-1} = (ts)^2$ is
parabolic, but its lifts to $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ carry the wrong sign. We have not
turned this into a proof.
\end{observation}

\section{Uniqueness of the Lattice Constraints: the Alpöge Matrices}

\subsection{The families}

Conjugating \eqref{eq:flag} by $U = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & x & z \\
0 & I & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ fixes $M$ and acts by
\begin{equation}
  r \mapsto r + x(M - I), \qquad c \mapsto c + (I - M)\,y,
  \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with the \emph{same} $x$ and $y$ on both generators (derived symbolically).
Since $M_2 - I$ is invertible over $\QQ$, with
$\det(M_2-I)=\underbrace{\det M_2}_{1}-\underbrace{\operatorname{tr}M_2}_{0}+1=2$
for the order-four block, the data of $T_2$ reduces to $2 \times 2$ residue classes --- and that
spends all of $x$ and $y$. Nothing is left to normalise $T_1$: its $r$ and
$c$ are genuinely free, and the extensions come in infinite families.
Given $r$ and $c$, the corner $s$ is fixed by $T^m = I$, whose corner reads
$ms + f(r,c) = 0$ and need not have an integer solution.

\begin{observation}[the near misses at the level of the lattice]
\label{obs:nearmiss}
Each of the four classes has a unique primitive invariant alternating form $Q$, of
Pfaffian $-6$ (type $(1,6)$, as the paper's $Q_0$); $N^2=0$ with $QN$ symmetric; the
form $b(x,y)=Q(x,Ny)$ on $V/\ker N$ is indefinite of discriminant $-6$; and $N$ has
Smith form $\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0,0)$ with $\operatorname{im}N=\ker N$, so the cusp
width is the same. $T_1$ and $T_2$ have characteristic polynomials
$(x-1)^2(x^2+x+1)$ and $(x-1)^2(x^2+1)$ in every class, so the rotation data at the
fixed points is the same. In a basis where $Q=Q_0$ the transformation laws of
Definition~3.1 have the shape $\bigl[\begin{smallmatrix}6\mu&\tau\\\beta&\mu\end{smallmatrix}\bigr]$
and close up ($g_1^3=g_2^4=g_0g_1g_2=\mathrm{id}$, exactly) for each class; the twist
never enters them. The one invariant that differs is the genus of $b$: it is
$\langle1,-6\rangle$ for the paper's class and for the class with $|p|=3$, and
$\langle2,-3\rangle$ for $|p|=2$ and $|p|=6$ (these are not equivalent:
$x^2-6y^2=-1$ has no solution modulo $3$, and $2x^2-3y^2=-1$ has one).
\end{observation}

So the lattice data does not separate the near misses from the paper's class, apart
from that genus, and the indefiniteness of $b$ holds for the paper's class too, so it
cannot be what decides viability. The equivalences of binary forms were worked by hand.

\section{The Fibre Structure and its Holonomy: a Gauge Group Derived from the Fibre}
\label{sec:gauge}

Gomes \cite{Gomes} proposes gauge theory without principal bundles: start from a vector
bundle whose fibres carry a structure, take the gauge group to be the automorphism group of
that structure, and recover the structure group of the usual presentation as a holonomy. We
use this as a recipe, not as a philosophy. The threefold $X$ is a family of complex
$2$-tori over $\mathbb P^1$ whose integral first homology $V=\ZZ^4$ is a flat local system
with monodromy $T_1,T_2$ at the two orbifold points and $T_0=(T_1T_2)^{-1}$ at the cusp.
That is a bundle of the required kind, with a discrete fibre, and the recipe asks three
finite questions: which structure does every fibre carry, what is its automorphism group,
and how much of that group does the holonomy reach? Each has an exact answer, computed in
\texttt{pyCICY.sixsphere\_gauge} (\SSGaChecks{} checks, \texttt{tests/test\_sixsphere\_gauge.py}).
We write $H=\langle T_1,T_2\rangle$ and work in the basis $(\gamma,u,w,\delta)$ of
\cref{eq:flag}.

\subsection{The structure}

\begin{proposition}[the fibre structure is a symplectic lattice with a marked vector]
\label{prop:gauge-structure}
The tensors of degree at most two that both $T_1$ and $T_2$ fix are, over $\QQ$: in $V$ the
vector $\gamma$; in $V^*$ the covector $\hat\delta$ (the coordinate on $V/W$); in $V\otimes V$
the symmetric $\gamma\otimes\gamma$ and the alternating bivector $\eta$ dual to $Q_0$; in
$V^*\otimes V^*$ the alternating form $Q_0$ and $\hat\delta\otimes\hat\delta$; in
$\operatorname{End}V$ the identity and $\gamma\otimes\hat\delta$. The dimensions of the five
invariant spaces are $\SSGaTensorDims$. Moreover $\hat\delta=Q_0(\gamma,\cdot)$.
So the whole list is generated by the pair $(Q_0,\gamma)$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Each space is the kernel of the stacked equations $(T_j-1)x=0$ on a tensor space, solved
exactly over $\QQ$. The form $Q_0$ is the one of \cite[Lemma~2.8]{Alpoge}, and it is
unique up to scale in $V^*\otimes V^*$ among alternating forms. The identity
$\hat\delta=Q_0(\gamma,\cdot)$ is read off from the first row of $Q_0$.
\end{proof}

The structure is thus a lattice with a symplectic form and a vector, $\gamma$ being
unimodularly paired with the top coordinate: $Q_0(\gamma,\delta)=1$. Nothing else of
degree two is canonical, which is what it means for the fibre to be \emph{only} that.

\subsection{The gauge group the structure derives}

Write $L(M)=\operatorname{diag}(1,M,1)$ for $M\in\SL_2(\ZZ)$, and $h(c_1,c_2;s)=h(c,s)$ for
\[
  h(c,s)=\begin{pmatrix}1&-6c_2&6c_1&s\\0&1&0&c_1\\0&0&1&c_2\\0&0&0&1\end{pmatrix},
  \qquad c\in\ZZ^2,\ s\in\ZZ .
\]

\begin{proposition}[the automorphism group is the integral Jacobi group]
\label{prop:gauge-group}
The group $\{g\in\GL_4(\ZZ):g\gamma=\gamma,\ g^{t}Q_0g=Q_0\}$ is
\[
  \mathcal J=\{L(M)\,h(c,s)\}\;\cong\;\SL_2(\ZZ)\ltimes\mathrm{Heis},\qquad
  h(c,s)h(c',s')=h\bigl(c+c',\,s+s'+6\,\omega(c,c')\bigr),
\]
with $\omega(c,c')=c_1c'_2-c_2c'_1$ and $L(M)h(c,s)L(M)^{-1}=h(Mc,s)$. Its centre
contains $z=h(0,0;1)\colon\delta\mapsto\delta+\gamma$. Both $T_1$ and $T_2$ lie in
$\mathcal J$:
\[
  T_1=L(M_1)\,h(-1,0;2),\quad T_2=L(M_2)\,h(0,-1;-3),\qquad
  M_1=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\ 
  M_2=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
An automorphism fixing $\gamma$ and preserving $Q_0$ preserves $\gamma^\perp=\langle\gamma,u,w\rangle$,
acts on $\gamma^\perp/\gamma$ symplectically, hence by some $M\in\SL_2(\ZZ)$, and moves
$\delta$ by some $(s,c)$; solving $g^tQ_0g=Q_0$ symbolically for the remaining entries gives
the displayed family, including the \emph{forced} top row $(-6c_2,6c_1)$. The law and the conjugation formula are matrix
identities, checked symbolically.
\end{proof}

The flag class of \cref{sec:flag} has the same shape but leaves the row $r$ free; the
structure \emph{forces} it. This is the gauge group that a principal-bundle presentation
would postulate, and here it comes out of the fibre.

\subsection{The holonomy has index \texorpdfstring{$\SSGaIndex$}{12}}

Let $\alpha\colon\SL_2(\ZZ)\to\ZZ/12$ be the abelianisation, with $S\mapsto 9$ and $T\mapsto 1$ (the presentation
$\langle S,T\mid S^4,(ST)^3S^{-2}\rangle$ is quoted, and the two relations are checked on
the values). Then $\alpha(M_2)=9$ and, as $M_1=TS^{-1}$, $\alpha(M_1)=4$. On the Heisenberg part let
\[
  f\bigl(h(c,s)\bigr)=s-6\,q(c)\pmod{12},\qquad q(c)=\begin{cases}0&c\equiv0\pmod2,\\1&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\]
the odd quadratic refinement of $\omega \bmod 2$.

\begin{theorem}[the holonomy is the kernel of a character]
\label{thm:gauge-index}
The map $\chi\bigl(L(M)h\bigr)=\alpha(M)+f(h)$ is a homomorphism $\mathcal J\to\ZZ/12$,
onto, with $\chi(T_1)=\SSGaChiOne$, $\chi(T_2)=\SSGaChiTwo$ and $\chi(z)=\SSGaChiZ$, and
\[
  H=\langle T_1,T_2\rangle=\ker\chi ,\qquad[\mathcal J:H]=\SSGaIndex .
\]
In particular the central translation $z^k\colon\delta\mapsto\delta+k\gamma$ lies in $H$ if
and only if $12\mid k$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\emph{$\chi$ is a homomorphism.} $f$ is a homomorphism on $\mathrm{Heis}$ because
$6\,\omega(c,c')\equiv 6\,[q(c+c')-q(c)-q(c')]\pmod{12}$, a statement about $c,c'$ mod $2$
(sixteen cases, all checked). It is conjugation-invariant, $f(h(Mc,s))=f(h(c,s))$, because
$q$ is invariant under $\GL_2(\mathbb F_2)$ (it equals $1$ on all three non-zero classes).
Then $\chi$ is a homomorphism on the semidirect product.
\emph{$H\subseteq\ker\chi$.} $\chi(T_1)=4+8$ and $\chi(T_2)=9+3$ (from $s=2$, $c$ odd, and $s=-3$, $c$ odd), both $0$ mod $12$.
\emph{$\ker\chi\subseteq H$.} $H$ maps onto $\SL_2(\ZZ)$, since $M_2=S$ and $M_1M_2=T$. Explicit words
in $a=T_1$, $A=T_1^{-1}$, $b=T_2$, $B=T_2^{-1}$ give $h(1,0;6)=\texttt{\SSGaWordHOne}$
(length $\SSGaWordLenOne$), $h(0,1;6)=\texttt{\SSGaWordHTwo}$ (length $\SSGaWordLenTwo$) and
$h(-1,0;6)=\texttt{\SSGaWordHMinus}$ (length $\SSGaWordLenMinus$), where the letters $a$ and $A$ stand for $T_1$ and $T_1^{-1}$, and so on; the product of the first and the last is $z^{12}$.
The elements $h$ of the Heisenberg group with $f(h)=0$ are generated by $h(1,0;6)$,
$h(0,1;6)$ and $z^{12}$ (an $h(c,s)$ with $f=0$ is $h(1,0;6)^{c_1}h(0,1;6)^{c_2}$ times a power of $z$, and $f$ kills the first two while $f(z^t)=t$), so $\ker\chi\cap\mathrm{Heis}\subseteq H$. Given $g\in\ker\chi$ choose $h'\in H$ with the same $\SL_2$ part; then $h'^{-1}g$ lies in $\ker\chi\cap\mathrm{Heis}\subseteq H$.
\end{proof}

In Gomes's terms the structure group that a principal-bundle presentation would postulate is
$\mathcal J$, and the holonomy reaches it only up to $\ZZ/12$: what is missed is exactly
the central translations $\delta\mapsto\delta+k\gamma$ with $12\nmid k$. The number twelve
is the order of the abelianisation of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$; it equals $m_1m_2=3\cdot4$ of \cref{eq:p}, which we note without
claiming a relation.

\begin{remark}[the untwisted lifts fail the cusp]
\label{rem:gauge-levi}
The obvious lifts of $M_1$ and $M_2$ to $\mathcal J$, namely $L(M_1)$ and $L(M_2)$, have orders $3$ and $4$ but
$\chi=\SSGaChiLeviOne$ and $\SSGaChiLeviTwo$; they generate a copy of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ which is a
complement of $\mathrm{Heis}$, and the cusp of their product is unipotent of rank
$\SSGaLeviRank$, not $2$. The Heisenberg parts of $T_1$ and $T_2$ are precisely what
cancels the character $\alpha$.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[the character is a theta multiplier]
\label{rem:gauge-eta}
The multiplier system of $\eta^2$ is a character of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ of order $12$, so it
generates the character group $\operatorname{Hom}(\SL_2(\ZZ),\ZZ/12)$. The odd refinement $q$ of $\omega\bmod2$ has the shape of an
odd theta characteristic. \Cref{sec:theta} computes this: $\chi$ is exactly the multiplier of $e(\beta/12)\theta_1/\eta$ on the
Jacobi group, and the holonomy is its invariance group (\cref{thm:theta}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{Zariski density, and the real group on the period domain}

\begin{proposition}[the closure of the holonomy is the real Jacobi group]
\label{prop:gauge-zariski}
The Lie algebra generated, under conjugation by $H$, by the logarithms of two unipotent
elements of $H$ (the cusp $T_0$ and the commutator $[T_2^2,T_1]$) has dimension
$\SSGaZariskiDim$, and so does the Lie algebra $\mathfrak{sl}_2\ltimes\mathfrak{heis}_3$ of the real
stabiliser of $(Q_0,\gamma)$ (dimension \SSGaStabDim). So the identity
component of the real Zariski closure of $H$ is the full real Jacobi group.
\end{proposition}

Feeding one-parameter subgroups of the real group through the routine that derives the
period laws from a matrix (\texttt{laws\_from\_lattice}) gives the classical Jacobi action on
$(\tau,\mu,\beta)$: $\mu\mapsto\mu+t$, $\mu\mapsto\mu-t\tau$, $\beta\mapsto\beta+t$, and
$\SL_2(\RR)$ by M\"obius maps. Each keeps the $6\mu$ shape and preserves
$D=\operatorname{Im}\beta-6(\operatorname{Im}\mu)^2/\operatorname{Im}\tau$. A point has an
orbit of real dimension $\SSGaOrbitDim$ and a circle for stabiliser (computed at
$(i,0,-i)$), so the level sets of $D$ are $G(\RR)/\mathrm{SO}(2)$. The compact group that
the structure-first reading derives at a point is therefore $U(1)$. These are computations on the
laws; they say nothing about whether the period functions exist.

\subsection{The four near misses are the four divisibilities of \texorpdfstring{$\gamma$}{gamma}}

\Cref{prop:classes} separates the \SSClassesWord{} classes by the values of $|p|$ modulo $12$ that
their twists reach. The structure gives a second invariant, and a cleaner one. Let $Q$ be the unique primitive invariant alternating form of a class
(Pfaffian $6$ in the basis $(\gamma,u,w,\delta)$; the sign of the Pfaffian depends on the orientation of the basis and is not a $\GL_4(\ZZ)$-invariant)
and let $d$ be the divisibility of the functional $Q(\gamma,\cdot)$. Since $Q$ is unique and $\gamma$ is canonical up to sign, $d$ is a
conjugacy invariant.

\begin{observation}[divisibility and $|p|$]
\label{obs:gauge-classes}
Over the $\SSGaSurvivors$ funnel survivors at box $3$ (a smaller box than the funnel of \cref{sec:forced}, for speed) the values of $d$ are $\SSGaDValues$, with
$\SSGaPerClass$ survivors each. On every survivor $d$ divides every reachable $p$, the greatest common divisor of the reachable $p$
is $d$, and the least non-zero $|p|$ is $d$. The paper's class is $d=1$, where $\gamma$ is paired
unimodularly by the structure. The central translations $h(0,0;s)$ found in the holonomy of the four classes
have greatest common $s$ equal to $\SSGaCentralIndex$ respectively, as in \cref{thm:gauge-index} (a word search over
$60\,000$ elements, so observed and not proved for the three near misses).
\end{observation}

The mechanism: $3\mid d$ forces $3\mid\ell_1$ and $2\mid d$ forces $\ell_2$ even, so
$3\mid p$, respectively $2\mid p$, by \cref{eq:p}. That is seen on the survivors, not proved in general. It explains why the three near misses bottom out at $|p|=\SSNearMissMins$ and it
agrees with the genus statement of \cref{obs:nearmiss}: $d=1,3$ against $d=2,6$.

\subsection{What this does and does not say}

The results of this section are statements about the lattice and the real group
that its structure fixes. They do not say that Gomes's philosophical claims are right or
wrong, only that his recipe can be run here and what it returns:
the derived group is $\mathcal J$, the holonomy is a subgroup of index $\SSGaIndex$,
real-Zariski dense, and its missing part is central. Nothing is claimed about the period functions
(so whether the near-miss classes carry complex manifolds stays declined), and the character
is identified with a theta multiplier in \cref{sec:theta}, as a statement about the laws and not about the threefold.

\section{Electrons on the Cusp Fibre, and the Hat as a Subgraph}
\label{sec:hofstadter}

\Cref{thm:laves} says that the strata of the cusp fibre have the incidence structure of the
$[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling rolled up on the cusp torus $\RR^2/B_0\ZZ^2$. A tiling is also a
graph, and a graph can carry an electron. We put one orbital on every site and a hopping $1$
on every bond, and ask what the A$_2$ structure behind the cusp fan does to the spectrum, how
a magnetic field enters without choosing a gauge on a tiling, and what the Hat, which sits
inside this graph, inherits. This is a model of an electron on a graph; the six-sphere knows
nothing of electrons (\texttt{pyCICY.cusp\_hofstadter}, \SSHoChecks{} checks, \texttt{tests/test\_cusp\_hofstadter.py}).

\subsection{The bands, in A$_2$ invariants}

Per fundamental domain there are six sites, a hexagon centre $C$ (a component of the cusp fibre), three
edge midpoints $E$ (the double curves) and two hexagon vertices $V$ (the triple points), joined
by twelve bonds, six $C$--$E$ and six $E$--$V$, and there are six kites. In lattice
coordinates the sites are torsion points of the hexagonal torus: $C=0$, the $E$ are the three
$2$-torsion points and the $V$ are $(\tfrac13,\tfrac13)$ and $(\tfrac23,\tfrac23)$.

\begin{proposition}[the Bloch polynomial is a function of the A$_2$ roots]
\label{prop:ho-bands}
With $x=e^{ik_1}$, $y=e^{ik_2}$,
\[
  \det\bigl(E-H(k)\bigr)=E^6-(12+s)\,E^4-3(s-6)\,E^2-\lvert\Delta\rvert^2,
\]
where $s=x+x^{-1}+y+y^{-1}+xy^{-1}+yx^{-1}$ is the sum over the six roots of $A_2$ and
$\Delta=(1-x)(1-y)(1-x/y)$ is the $A_2$ Weyl denominator. Hence $E=0$ is an eigenvalue exactly on the three mirror
lines $k_1=0$, $k_2=0$, $k_1=k_2$ of the Weyl group acting on the Brillouin torus: nodal
\emph{lines}, not Dirac points. At $k=0$ the polynomial is $E^4(E^2-18)$, and the whole spectrum lies in
$[-3\sqrt2,3\sqrt2]$, the end points being attained at $k=0$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The Hamiltonian is bipartite, $E$ against $C+V$, so the polynomial is a cubic in $E^2$ whose
constant term is $-\lvert\det B\rvert^2$ for the $3\times3$ block $B$ from the $E$ sites to the others;
$\det B$ is $x^{-1}\Delta$, a unit times the Weyl denominator, and the other two coefficients are
expanded symbolically. The constant term vanishes exactly when $\Delta=0$, that is when $x=1$, $y=1$ or $x=y$. At $k=0$ the
row sums of $BB^t$ are all $18$ ($=4+2\cdot5+2\cdot2$: the degree, two hexagon cliques and two
triangle cliques). Entrywise, $\lvert H(k)\rvert=H(0)$, so the spectral radius of $H(k)$ is at most that of $H(0)$,
which is $\sqrt{18}$.
\end{proof}

The six bands, read from a $24\times24$ grid of $k$ (and not proved), are
$[-3\sqrt2,-\sqrt3]$, $[-2,0]$, $[-\sqrt3,0]$, $[0,\sqrt3]$, $[0,2]$ and $[\sqrt3,3\sqrt2]$. They overlap, so the zero-field spectrum has no
gap. That the triangulation behind the cusp fan (\cref{sec:laves}) reappears here as the root
system of $A_2$ in the spectrum is a consequence of the Laves structure, and we draw no further moral.

\subsection{A field as a connection, with no gauge chosen on the tiling}

A uniform field with flux $\phi$ through each kite is a connection on the substrate, written
in a Landau gauge and checked kite by kite. On the cusp torus it is a line bundle exactly when the flux per
domain, $6\phi$, is an integer, which is its Chern number. (The torus here is the one on which the substrate is rolled up in \cref{sec:rollup}, not the
fibre $W$ itself.) The point is what happens on a subgraph. Restricting the Peierls phases of the substrate to a
subgraph is restricting a connection, and nothing else is needed. This is the
order of construction of \cref{sec:gauge}: an aperiodic tiling has no torus, hence no principal bundle to start from, and
nothing is lost without one.

\begin{observation}[the Hat is a subgraph]
\label{obs:ho-subgraph}
At $a:b=\sqrt3:1$ every Hat is eight whole kites and every mystic ten
(\cref{sec:rollup}), and every edge of the $14$-edge form of the Hat is a single kite edge ($C$--$E$ of
length $\sqrt3$, $E$--$V$ of length $1$). So the vertex graph of a Hat tiling is the substrate with the bonds
inside tiles deleted, and its tight-binding model in a field is the restriction of the substrate's. On the generated level-$3$ patch
of the repository (\texttt{paper/make\_sixsphere\_hat\_hofstadter.py}) there are $443$ tiles,
$2948$ vertices and $3390$ edges, with degrees in $\{2,3,4\}$ and $V-E+F=1$.
\end{observation}

\begin{proposition}[the periods in the flux]
\label{prop:ho-period}
The spectrum of a tight-binding model with Peierls phases on a planar graph depends, up to
unitary equivalence, only on the fluxes through its bounded faces modulo $1$. The faces of the substrate are kites, so
its gauge period in $\phi$ is $1$. The bounded faces of a Hat graph are Hats and mystics, carrying fluxes $8\phi$ and $10\phi$, so its period is
$1/\gcd(8,10)=\SSHoPeriodHat$: at $\phi=\tfrac12$ the Hat model is gauge-equivalent to zero field,
while the substrate carries $\pi$ flux through every kite. The Spectre is not a union of kites (\cref{sec:proportions}) and has no
such statement.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On a plane graph the cycle space is generated by the bounded faces, so two connections with equal face fluxes mod $1$ differ by
a gauge transformation. Shifting $\phi$ by $t$ shifts the face fluxes by $8t$ and $10t$; both are integers
iff $t\in\tfrac12\ZZ$ (their difference is $2t$).
\end{proof}

\subsection{Chern numbers by two routes}

For $\phi=p/q$ the magnetic Bloch bundle of a $q\times1$ supercell has a Chern number in every gap, computed by the
projector formula of Fukui, Hatsugai and Suzuki (FHS), from overlaps of eigenvectors and with no gauge
fixed. Each gap satisfies the St\v{r}eda/TKNN relation $\rho=\sigma\cdot6\phi+s$ with $s$
an integer ($\rho$ the number of states per domain below the gap, $\sigma$ the Chern number), in all $\SSHoGapsTotal$ gaps at $\phi=\tfrac13,\tfrac14,\tfrac16$.

Independently, Kitaev's real-space formula
$\nu=12\pi i\sum_{j\in A,k\in B,l\in C}(P_{jk}P_{kl}P_{lj}-P_{jl}P_{lk}P_{kj})$, with $P$ the projector

\subsection{The Hat in a field}

On the level-$3$ Hat patch of \cref{obs:ho-subgraph}, the same real-space marker resolves
\emph{no} Hofstadter gap with a non-zero Chern number: over a grid of $41$ values of the flux
and $141$ of the Fermi energy it forms no integer plateau, its largest value being $0.93$ at isolated points inside bands, while the substrate under it has clean
$\pm1$ gaps over wide ranges of flux. Deleting the bonds inside tiles, which here removes about half
of them (the patch keeps $46\%$), closes them. This is a measurement on one finite patch, quoted from the repository; small-flux Landau gaps would need patches larger than the
magnetic length and are not excluded.

\subsection{What this says}

The bands, the periods and the Chern numbers of the substrate are exact, or reach the same
integer by two independent routes, and what the Hat inherits by restriction follows from
\cref{obs:ho-subgraph}. Nothing here is a property of the six-sphere: the electron is a model on the graph of
a tiling that the cusp fibre happens to be rolled up from, and the Hat's gaps are as measured on a patch.

\section{The Holonomy Character as a Theta Multiplier}
\label{sec:theta}

\Cref{rem:gauge-eta} observed that $\chi=\alpha+f$ has the shape of a theta multiplier. It is one, and this can be
proved from the classical transformation laws. On the period domain, with the coordinates
$(\tau,\mu,\beta)$ of the period matrix $Z=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}6\mu&\tau\\\beta&\mu\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, put
\[
  F(\tau,\mu,\beta)=e\!\left(\tfrac{\beta}{12}\right)\frac{\theta_1(\tau,\mu)}{\eta(\tau)},\qquad e(x)=e^{2\pi ix},
\]
with $\theta_1(\tau,z)$ the odd Jacobi theta function whose nome is $e(\tau/2)$. The quotient $\theta_1/\eta$ is a
Jacobi form of weight $0$ and index $\tfrac12$ \cite{EichlerZagier}; the factor $e(-c\mu^2/2(c\tau+d))$ that the index produces under
$\tau\mapsto(a\tau+b)/(c\tau+d)$ is what $\beta$ absorbs, since $\beta$ transforms with $-6c\mu^2/(c\tau+d)$
(for $L(S)$: $\beta\mapsto\beta-6\mu^2/\tau$). Each element of $\mathcal J$ acts on $(\tau,\mu,\beta)$ by the law that
its integer matrix alone forces (\texttt{pyCICY.sixsphere\_theta}, \SSThChecks{} checks, \texttt{tests/test\_sixsphere\_theta.py}).

\begin{theorem}[the holonomy--multiplier theorem]
\label{thm:theta}
For every $g\in\mathcal J$,
\[
  F(g\cdot x)=e\!\left(\tfrac{\chi(g)}{12}\right)F(x),
\]
with no automorphy factor. Consequently the stabiliser of $F$ in $\mathcal J$ is $\ker\chi=\langle T_1,T_2\rangle$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
It suffices to treat five generators, since $\chi$ is a homomorphism (\cref{thm:gauge-index}), the action is a group action,
and $S$, $T$ and the three Heisenberg generators generate $\mathcal J$. The classical laws are, for $\theta_1$ and $\eta$ \cite{Mumford}:
\begin{align*}
  \theta_1(-1/\tau,\mu/\tau)&=-i\sqrt{-i\tau}\;e(\mu^2/2\tau)\,\theta_1(\tau,\mu), & \eta(-1/\tau)&=\sqrt{-i\tau}\,\eta(\tau),\\
  \theta_1(\tau+1,\mu)&=e(1/8)\,\theta_1(\tau,\mu), & \eta(\tau+1)&=e(1/24)\,\eta(\tau),\\
  \theta_1(\tau,\mu+1)&=-\theta_1(\tau,\mu), & \theta_1(\tau,\mu-\tau)&=-e(\mu-\tau/2)\,\theta_1(\tau,\mu).
\end{align*}
Adding to each the shift of $\beta$ in the law of the generator gives the table below. Every sum is a constant (no $\tau$, $\mu$ or $\beta$ is left), the
$\sqrt{-i\tau}$ of $\theta_1$ and $\eta$ cancel, and $12$ times the constant is $\chi$ of the generator mod $12$.
\end{proof}

\centering
\begin{tabular}{@{}lllllc@{}}

\midrule
$L(S)$ & $(-1/\tau,\ \mu/\tau,\ \beta-6\mu^2/\tau)$ & $-i\,e(\mu^2/2\tau)$ & $e(-\mu^2/2\tau)$ & $-i=e(\tfrac9{12})$ & $9$ \\

The theorem was also measured, independently of the proof. At \SSThDigits{} digits (with $\theta_1$ summed from its own series, because a library routine
with nome $e(\tau/2)$ picks a branch of $q^{1/4}$ that gives the wrong multiplier for $T$), the multiplier of each of the five generators
and of $T_1$, $T_2$ is $e(\chi/12)$ with modulus $1$ and deviation below $\SSThMaxDev$, and the same holds on \SSThWords{} random words in
them. The character $\alpha$ obtained from a Euclid decomposition of $M$ into $S$ and $T$ agrees with the one read off the $\eta^2$ multiplier
$\eta(M\tau)^2/((c\tau+d)\eta(\tau)^2)$ numerically, a route that never decomposes $M$, on \SSThAlphaAgree{} of \SSThAlphaOf{} random words of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$. The
classical laws quoted above hold against the series with relative error below $\SSThClassicalErr$.

\subsection{What it gives}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{A second proof of index $12$, without search.} The stabiliser of $F$ is $\ker\chi$, and $e(\chi/12)$ takes $12$ values:
the powers $z^k$, $k=0,\dots,11$, reach all \SSThCosets{} of them. So $F^{12}$ is invariant under all of $\mathcal J$.
\item \emph{The holonomy is the invariance group of one explicit function.} In the terms of \cref{sec:gauge}, the structure that $H$ preserves beyond $(Q_0,\gamma)$
is $F$, up to the twelfth roots of unity.
\item \emph{The two halves of $\chi$ are the two factors of $F$.} $\alpha$ is the multiplier of $\theta_1/\eta$ on $\SL_2(\ZZ)$, which is that of $\eta^2$
because $\theta_1(\tau,\mu)/\eta(\tau)=2\pi\mu\,\eta(\tau)^2+O(\mu^3)$. $f$ is the multiplier of the Heisenberg translations,
the odd characteristic of $\theta_1$. That is why $q$ had to be the \emph{odd} refinement in \cref{sec:gauge}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The controls, and the zeros}

The candidates $e(k\beta)\,\theta_j(\tau,\pm\mu)\,\eta(\tau)^r$ with $k=\pm\tfrac1{12}$, $j=1,\dots,4$ and $r\in\{-1,1,-3,2,-2\}$ number
\SSThCandidates. Exactly \SSThSurvivors{} are fixed by $T_1$ and $T_2$: $k=\tfrac1{12}$, $j=1$, $r=-1$, with either sign of $\mu$ ($\theta_1$ is odd). The even thetas carry the wrong
characteristic, and $e(\beta/6)\,\theta_1/\eta$, whose $\beta$-coefficient is doubled, is not fixed by $T_1$.

$F$ vanishes exactly where $\theta_1$ does, on $\mu\in\ZZ+\tau\ZZ$, and (checked at the base point) it is non-zero at the other three half
periods. What this divisor is on the period domain, and whether it meets the degeneration, is open.

\begin{remark}[a physics reading, not used]
\label{rem:theta-fermi}
In the elliptic genus of a two-dimensional $\mathcal N=(0,2)$ theory, a Fermi multiplet of flavour charge $1$ contributes
$i\,\theta_1(\tau,z)/\eta(\tau)$, with $z$ the flavour fugacity, and a chiral multiplet its reciprocal \cite[eqs.~(2.13), (2.14)]{BEHT}. So the
factor $\theta_1(\tau,\mu)/\eta(\tau)$ of $F$ has the shape of a single Fermi multiplet's. That is all this remark says. The six-sphere
has no such theory attached to it, $\mu$ is a period coordinate and not a fugacity, and we do not interpret $e(\beta/12)$; the reading is a coincidence of
formulas until a theory is exhibited.
\end{remark}

\subsection{What this does and does not say}

\Cref{thm:theta} is a statement about a function on the period domain of the \emph{laws} $(\tau,\mu,\beta)$. Whether those functions exist on
the threefold is the analytic question that the paper declines, so $F$ is a function on the model, not on $X$. The theorem is exact once the five classical laws are
granted, and they are quoted; the Jacobi-form phrasing is from the literature \cite{EichlerZagier} and was not rederived.

\section{Orientation Reversal and Lattice Duality}
\label{sec:mirror}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:achiral}
Reversing the orientation of the base replaces $(T_1, T_2)$ by
$(T_1^{-1}, T_2^{-1})$ \cite[Remark~3.18]{Alpoge}. The two pairs are
conjugate:
\[
  P = \SSMirrorConj, \qquad \det P = \SSMirrorDet,
\]
satisfies $P T_j P^{-1} = T_j^{-1}$ for $j = 1, 2$.
\end{proposition}

The monodromy therefore cannot distinguish $X$ from its orientation-reversed
counterpart. This is a statement about the lattice, not a proof that $X$ is
biholomorphic to its conjugate. It is machine-checked (\texttt{lattice\_achiral}). The involution is orientation reversal of the base; it preserves the reachable $|p|$ residues $\{1,5\}$ (as it must, since $|p|$ is insensitive to sign), and the lattice data is a fixed point, in contrast with the Spectre, which is strictly chiral~\cite{SmithSpectre}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:dual}
The monodromy lattice $V$ and its dual are isomorphic over $\QQ$ but not
over $\ZZ$: every integral intertwiner has determinant $\SSDualDetForm$ in
the coordinates $(s, t)$ of the rank-two lattice of intertwiners.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The invariant alternating form $Q_0$ of \cite[Lemma~2.8]{Alpoge} satisfies
$Q_0 T = T^{-t} Q_0$, so it intertwines $V$ and its dual over $\QQ$; but
$\det Q_0 = \SSDetQZero$. The intertwiners form a rank-two lattice, and the
determinant of the general one factors as $\SSDualDetForm$, which is never
$\pm 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{sloppypar}
Alpöge shows the construction carries no polarization, because the form
$b(x, y) = Q_0(x, Ny)$ is indefinite. \Cref{prop:dual} is a sharper,
integral statement: there is not even a non-positive \emph{principal} one.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Arithmetic Invariants and Resemblances to the Spectre}
\label{sec:arith}

The special values of the construction's period functions lie at the
elliptic points, $\tau(z_1) = e^{i\pi/3}$ and $\tau(z_2) = i$, generating
$\QQ(\sqrt{-3}, i) = \QQ(\zeta_{12})$. Its quadratic square classes are
$\SSClassesPeriods$ --- signed: dropping the sign would report the classes of the real field $\QQ(\sqrt3)$
and manufacture an identity with the monotile's geometry. With signs kept:

\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule

\midrule
six-sphere periods, $\QQ(\zeta_{12})$ & $\SSClassesPeriods$ & --- \\
monotile geometry, $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ & $\{1, 3\}$ & $\SSShareGeometry$ \\

The six-sphere shares the monotiles' \emph{geometry} field and none of their
\emph{inflation} fields --- consistent with \cref{sec:laves}, where it shares
the substrate and not the tiling.

Two further facts sharpen the picture. The Euclidean triangle groups are
$(2,3,6)$, $(2,4,4)$ and $(3,3,3)$; none contains both a $3$ and a $4$, so
the base orbifold of the construction is necessarily hyperbolic, while the
substrate's symmetry is the Euclidean $(2,3,6)$. And the cusp component's
polygon is the hexagon of the toric surface $B_3$, whose hopping vectors are
those of the triangular lattice: the mirror curve of the component is the
triangular-lattice Hofstadter model of~\cite{chen2025,shaffer2022}.

\paragraph{The $\sqrt6$.} The form $b = 6w^2 - \delta^2$ has isotropic
slopes $\pm\sqrt6$, and the Spectre's inflation factor is $\lambda =
(\sqrt6 + \sqrt{10})/2$, with $\lambda^2 = 4 + \sqrt{15}$: the two share the
square class $\SSShareLatticeSpectre$. The origins are unrelated. The
six-sphere's $6$ is $Q_0(u,w)$, forced by the entry $-6$ of $T_1$, which the period laws pin down; the Spectre's is a consequence of
its substitution matrix. Nothing downstream of either uses the other.

\paragraph{Valence four.} The Laves valences $(6, 4, 3)$ echo the orbifold
orders $(3, 4, \infty)$. But the valence-four sites are the double curves of
$W$, which lie over the cusp $p_0$ --- not over $p_2$, the point of order
four, whose fibre is a multiple fibre with no double curves at all.

\subsection{Two more matches that are not connections}
\label{sec:cosmo-nolink}

Two further coincidences of numbers, both with a cosmological singularity, are exact and
are not connections. They are computed in \texttt{pyCICY.sixsphere\_cosmology}, and all
$\SSCoChecks{}$ of its checks pass.

\paragraph{The Mixmaster map.} The BKL map of the Mixmaster universe, $u\mapsto u-1$ for
$u\ge2$ and $u\mapsto1/(u-1)$ for $1<u<2$ \cite{BKL70}, is the Gauss map, so a closed orbit is a
periodic continued fraction and its multiplier is the square of the unit of the matrix
that the partial quotients multiply to. The Spectre's inflation $\lambda^2=4+\sqrt{15}$
is that unit for the period $(6,1)$: the orbit of $u^*=3+\sqrt{15}=[6;1,6,1,\dots]$ closes
after $\SSCoPeriod{}$ epochs, in eras of $\SSCoEras{}$ epochs, with multiplier
$(4+\sqrt{15})^2=\lambda^4\approx\SSCoMultNum$ exactly; $\lambda^2$ is an eigenvalue of
$\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}7&6\\1&1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$, of characteristic polynomial $x^2-8x+1$.
The growth rate $\nu=2+\sqrt5=\varphi^3=[4;4,\dots]$ of the supertile outline is the one-era orbit
of $\SSCoNuPeriod{}$ epochs, with multiplier $-\nu^2\approx-\SSCoNuMultNum$, the sign being
the determinant $-1$ of $\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}4&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$. The dimension of the
outline is therefore $D=\ln\nu/\ln\lambda=2\ln|m_\nu|/\ln m_\lambda=\SSCoDim$, a ratio of the
logarithmic multipliers of two closed Mixmaster orbits. This holds for \emph{every} periodic
continued fraction (checked over $\SSCoGridChecked{}$ periods), so it is a theorem about
continued fractions and not a property of the monotile, and there is no dynamics on the
tiling for it to describe. One more fact is a coincidence in the strict sense: the Kasner
denominator $1+u+u^2=|u-\zeta_3|^2$ is an Eisenstein norm, on the lattice $\mathbb Z[\zeta_3]$ of the
cusp torus, and at $u^*$ it equals $7\lambda^2$ with $7$ the number of epochs. That is a one-off:
it is rational over the unit at no other starting point checked ($\SSCoOneOffStarts{}$ of
$\SSCoOneOffStarts{}$), so it is left unexplained.

\paragraph{The pancake.} In the non-singular pancake of Dechant, Lasenby and
Hobson~\cite{DLH08} the collapsing fibre winds by $(2/a_0)\ln(t/t_{\rm in})$ over an Euler-angle
period of $4\pi$ (the identification of that period is not checked). Reading one substitution level as a
rescaling by $\lambda$ gives $2\pi a_0/\ln\lambda$ levels per fibre turn, and at $a_0=1$ that
is $\SSCoLevelsPerTurn{}$, within $\SSCoLevelsMissPercent\%$ of the $6$ of $\mathbb Z/6$ and not equal to it: exactly
$6$ would need $a_0=\SSCoAzeroSix{}$, a value chosen to give the answer, with $a_0$ free initial data. This is
numerology and is asserted as a miss. The group-theoretic half is exact and was in part already
here. The rotation by $60^\circ$ of the hexagonal cusp torus has stabilisers of orders
$\SSCoStabOrders{}$ on the six site classes (the centre; the vertices; the edge midpoints, which are the
double curves), so the torus modulo $\mathbb Z/6$ is the orbifold sphere $S^2(2,3,6)$ of Euler characteristic
$\SSCoOrbifoldChi{}$, the Euclidean $(2,3,6)$ group already named as the substrate's symmetry. The same
$\mathbb Z/6$ in the fibre circle gives the lens space $L(6,1)$ over the pancake's base. Sharing the group is
the whole of the overlap: no map from the six fibre classes to the pancake's fibre is constructed.

\section{An Integer Inflation on the Same Kind of Torus: Chair44}
\label{sec:chair44}

\Cref{sec:rollup} ends with a negative result: no Hat tiling descends to $\TT$,
and a four-step Hat patch agrees with no non-trivial lattice translate of itself
beyond $0.796$ (\cref{fig:overlap}). That is what an aperiodic tiling does when
its inflation factor is irrational. Tsiokos's \emph{Chair44}~\cite{Tsiokos1}
is an aperiodic tile of the other kind. It is a seven-cube chair, the
$2\times2\times2$ cube minus a corner, decorated so that it is claimed to tile only
aperiodically, and its substitution has eight children and inflation factor the
\emph{integer} $2$. That the decorated tile is aperiodic, and an einstein tile, is
Tsiokos's claimed proof, which we quote. That every tiling lies in the substitution hull
is a theorem of Romanov, in the joint paper~\cite{HartshornRomanov} (it is conditional on
Tsiokos's registration of tilings and on a unique parent tiling), which we also quote and do
not re-derive. Chair44 is not the only 2026 tile in three dimensions with a global property forced by a local one:
the OpenAI preprint~\cite{OAI155} gives a finite translational tile in $\ZZ^3$ that tiles but has no fully periodic tiling.
It allows translations only and has no inflation, so it has no tower of tori to descend to, and we do not use it. What we compute is a finite and exact comparison, in \texttt{pyCICY.chair44\_nested}, which carries its own copy of the
eight child poses and re-checks that they tile each of the $24$ rotated doubled
chairs. The six-sphere contains no Chair44; the section sets two kinds of tiling
side by side on tori.

\subsection{Why nested tori}
\label{sec:chair-nested}

An integer inflation is a lattice map. A level-$M$ supertile is a Chair44 scaled by
$2^M$, so level-$M$ supertiles are placed on the sublattice $2^M\ZZ^3\subset\ZZ^3$
of index $8^M$, and a level-$M$ block of unit cells has $8^M$ cells. The natural
family of tori is $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$, each covering the last. The Hat's and the
Spectre's inflations are irrational (\cref{sec:arith}), so they leave no such
family.

This echoes, as a count and nothing more, Remark~4.9 of~\cite{Alpoge}, as reproduced
for the cusp fibre in \cref{sec:laves}: a sublattice of index $d$ of the cusp lattice is
$d$ copies of the fundamental domain, giving $d$ components and $e(W)=2d$. In the plane
the nested sublattices would have index $4^M$; for Chair44 they have index $8^M$. The cusp
fibre is two-dimensional, no complex fourfold with a Chair44 cusp fibre is constructed
here, and none is claimed.

\subsection{Coincidence, and its rate}
\label{sec:chair-rate}

The substitution acts on the alphabet $\mathcal A$ of $\SSChLabels{}=24\times7$
labelled unit cells (a rotation and a cell of the chair): $\sigma$ sends a label to the
eight labels of its $2\times2\times2$ block, every column sum of $\sigma$ is $8$, and
$\sigma$ is primitive. For $a\in\{0,\dots,2^M-1\}^3$ let $\sigma^M(\ell)(a)$ be the label
of the cell at address $a$ inside the block of $\ell$ after $M$ steps. The address $a$
is \emph{coincident} at depth $M$ if $\sigma^M(\ell)(a)$ does not depend on $\ell$.
Let $c(M)$ be the number of addresses that are not, and
$E(M)=\sum_a\bigl(\lvert\{\sigma^M(\ell)(a):\ell\in\mathcal A\}\rvert-1\bigr)$. The least
depth with a coincident address is $\SSChCoincDepth{}$, and then $\SSChCoincAddr{}$ of
the $\SSChCoincTotal{}$ addresses are coincident, as recorded upstream
(\cite{Tsiokos1}, the modular coincidence).

\begin{proposition}[the rate]
\label{prop:chair-rate}
For every $M\ge1$,
\[
c(M)=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst,\qquad E(M)=\SSChECoef\cdot2^M+\SSChEConst .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
$M$ & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\
\midrule
addresses $8^M$ & \SSChRowAddr \\
$c(M)$ & \SSChRowC \\
$E(M)$ & \SSChRowE \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proof}
Reading the digits of an address from the most significant, the set of labels still
possible is obtained from $\mathcal A$ by applying $M$ of the eight maps
$A\mapsto\{\sigma(\ell)_b:\ell\in A\}$. The reachable sets form an automaton with
$\SSChSets{}$ states, $\SSChNonSingleton{}$ of which have more than one element. A
singleton goes to a singleton, so $c(M)$ and $E(M)$ are linear functionals of $T^Me_0$,
with $T$ the transfer matrix on the non-singleton states. By Cayley--Hamilton each
satisfies a linear recurrence of order at most $\SSChNonSingleton{}$; the closed form
satisfies one of order two, with roots $2$ and $1$. Their difference satisfies a
recurrence of order at most $\SSChNonSingleton{}+2=\SSChTermsNeeded{}$, and vanishes for
all $M\ge1$ as soon as it vanishes for $\SSChTermsNeeded{}$ consecutive values. It
vanishes for $M=1,\dots,\SSChTermsChecked{}$, checked in exact integer arithmetic.
\end{proof}

The closed form for $c$ holds from $M=1$ and fails at $M=0$. The fraction of undetermined
addresses, $c(M)/8^M\sim28\cdot4^{-M}$, tends to zero.

\subsection{How nearly the tiling descends}
\label{sec:chair-descent}

\begin{proposition}[descent to the nested tori]
\label{prop:chair-descent}
Let $P$ be a finite patch that is a union of whole level-$M$ blocks, the block at
$2^My$, $y\in Y$, carrying the labels $\sigma^M(\ell_y)$. For $v\in\ZZ^3$, among the cells
$x\in P$ with $x+2^Mv\in P$, the labels at $x$ and $x+2^Mv$ differ for at most a fraction
$c(M)/8^M$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such cells are $x=2^My+a$ with $y,y+v\in Y$ and $a$ an address. The labels at $x$ and
$x+2^Mv$ are $\sigma^M(\ell_y)(a)$ and $\sigma^M(\ell_{y+v})(a)$, which agree whenever $a$
is coincident, whatever the two parent labels are. At most $c(M)$ of the $8^M$ addresses
are not.
\end{proof}

The proposition has no experiment in it. The patch $P_N$ obtained by applying $\sigma^N$
to the seven cells of one chair is a union of whole level-$M$ blocks for every $M\le N$.
For $N=6$ it has $\SSChPatchCells{}$ cells, and the inequality holds on all
$\SSChAgreeRows{}$ translates tested, three axes and $M=1,\dots,5$. Along $x$:

\subsection{What remains undetermined}
\label{sec:chair-undet}

The transfer matrix of \cref{prop:chair-rate} on the $\SSChNonSingleton{}$ non-singleton
sets has characteristic polynomial $\SSChCharPoly$, and spectral radius exactly $2$.
Since $c(M)\sim2^M$ inside $8^M$ addresses, the set of addresses where the label is not
yet determined has box dimension $\log_22=1$ in the $2$-adic internal space $\ZZ_2^3$, in
keeping with window boundaries of measure zero. The tower of tori $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$ is the finite shadow of that
internal space, the inverse limit of the groups $\ZZ^3/2^M\ZZ^3$. The $p$-adic exclusion theorem of the OpenAI preprint~\cite{OAIHS},
that a jointly continuous action of $\ZZ_p$ on a connected manifold has a nonzero kernel, does not constrain it: the hull of an
aperiodic tiling is locally a Euclidean factor times a Cantor set and not a manifold. We name it only because $2$-adic groups appear in both.

\begin{observation}[six candidate rotations]
\label{obs:chair-six}
At $M=\SSChUndetM$, of the $8^{\SSChUndetM}$ addresses $\SSChUndet{}$ are undetermined.
Of these, $\SSChSix{}$ carry exactly six candidate labels, whose six rotations are in
every case a coset of an order-six subgroup of the rotation group of the cube (one
identity, three half-turns, two third-turns, all fixing a body diagonal as an axis), and
$\SSChTwentyFour{}$ carry $24$ labels.
\end{observation}

The substitution thus fixes the orientation of such a cell up to the dihedral group of a
body diagonal, which contains the symmetry the undecorated chair has about its own
diagonal. Where the undetermined addresses sit is also exactly describable, and that
explains the number six.

\begin{proposition}[the undetermined set is four tubes]
\label{prop:chair-tubes}
For every $M=1,\dots,\SSChTubeM$, the addresses of the $2^M$-cube at which the label is not
yet determined are exactly the union of four \emph{tubes}, one along each body diagonal. A
tube is the set of cells $(x,y,z)$ whose difference $(x-z,\,y-z)$ is that of one of the
seven cells of the chair: the chair swept along its notch diagonal, with $7\cdot2^M-6$
cells. The four tubes have exactly eight cells in common, the central $2\times2\times2$ block
of the cube, and each of these lies in all four tubes, so the tubes cover the union
$3\cdot8=\SSChTubeOverlap$ fewer times than the sum of their sizes counts it. Hence
\[
  c(M)=4\,(7\cdot2^M-6)-\SSChTubeOverlap=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst,
  \qquad \SSChCCoef=4\times7.
\]
The label excess is $E(M)=\SSChECoef\cdot2^M+\SSChEConst$ for every $M\ge1$. For
$M\ge2$ it is five for each six-label address plus $23$ for each of the sixteen $24$-label
addresses; at $M=1$ the eight undetermined addresses instead carry sets of $42$ labels each.
\end{proposition}

\noindent
\emph{Status.} The identity of the two sets, and both counts, are checked for every
$M\le\SSChTubeM$ against the subset automaton (\texttt{chair44\_nested.undetermined\_is\_diagonal\_tubes},
one of the $\SSChChecks{}$ checks). That the tubes lie in the undetermined set for every $M$ is
the \emph{chair stack} of the joint paper~\cite{HartshornRomanov}: six labels $j$ with
$\sigma(j)_{000}=\sigma(j)_{111}=j$ whose chairs form a stack along the notch diagonal, each
filling the notch of the next, so that each of the seven cells of the chair gives a diagonal
line of identical labels in $\sigma^n(j)$ for every $n$ (an induction of $14$ finite cases). Six different frames put six different labels on
the same cell. The converse, that nothing else is undetermined, is the finite check, not a
proof for all $M$.

The six frames of those six labels are exactly the frames with notch $\pm(1,1,1)$, that is,
the stabiliser of a body diagonal, the order-six dihedral group of
\cref{obs:chair-six}. So the mechanism for the six candidate rotations is that the label is
undetermined where a stack can sit, and a stack can be rotated about its diagonal. The number
six is still not related to the six mystic fibre classes of \cref{sec:mystic}, where it counts
the oriented edges of the dual complex; we record that as a coincidence.

\subsection{Two defects of one hierarchy}
\label{sec:chair-defects}

The mismatch $c(M)$ was measured on a torus. The same substitution has a second, geometric
defect, the interface between supertiles, and the two have different exponents. Refine one
chair $n$ times. The level-$n$ patch has $8^n$ chairs and fills the chair scaled by $2^n$, that is $7\cdot8^n$
cells with no overlap (checked for every $n\le\SSChIfaceN{}$). Count, at the level of cells, the faces
$F_n$ between cells of different chairs, the boundary faces $B_n$ of the patch, and the number
$C_n$ of pairs of chairs in face contact.

\begin{proposition}[the interface]
\label{prop:chair-interface}
For $n=1,\dots,\SSChIfaceN{}$,
\[
F_n=\SSChFaceCoef\,(8^n-4^n),\qquad B_n=\SSChBoundCoef\cdot4^n,\qquad
C_n=\SSChContactVol\cdot8^n-\SSChContactArea\cdot4^n-\SSChContactConst,
\]
and the extensivity defect is $8C_n-C_{n+1}=-(\SSChDefectCoef\cdot4^n+\SSChDefectConst)$.
\end{proposition}

\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
$n$ & 1 & 2 & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 \\
\midrule
chairs $8^n$ & \SSChIfaceRowChairs \\
$F_n$ & \SSChIfaceRowF \\
$B_n$ & \SSChIfaceRowB \\
$C_n$ & \SSChIfaceRowC \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\emph{Status.} Counted, not proved for all $n$. Given that the patch is the scaled chair, $F_n$ and
$B_n$ are geometry: the scaled chair has volume $7\cdot8^n$ and surface $24\cdot4^n$ (the chair has the
surface of its $2\times2\times2$ cube, with the notch replacing the corner), there are $21\cdot8^n-12\cdot4^n$
adjacent pairs of cells in all, and nine inside each chair, which leaves $F_n$. The formula for $C_n$ is
read off the counts at $n=1,2,3$ and holds at every level computed.

Three exponents now sit side by side, in the linear size $L=2^n$ of a supertile. The bulk grows like
$8^n=L^3$. The interface has a term of order $4^n=L^2$, an area, which is the generic
boundary term of a count on a patch with free boundary. The coincidence count of
\cref{prop:chair-rate} has no such term: the characteristic polynomial of its transfer matrix
has only the roots $0$, $1$ and $2$, so $c(M)$ is of order $2^M=L$. A line, in keeping with
\cref{prop:chair-tubes}: the tubes have a fixed cross-section of $7$ cells and length $L$. Against the fraction
$24\cdot4^M/(7\cdot8^M)$ of a level-$M$ supertile that lies on its surface, the undetermined fraction
$c(M)/8^M$ is smaller by a factor that tends to $\SSChLineToSurface\cdot2^{-M}$. A
translate by $2^Mv$ therefore fails to agree not along the faces of the supertiles, which would cost a relative
$2^{-M}$, but only along their diagonals, at a cost of $4^{-M}$.

This is a comparison of exponents and no more. The interface count is a free-boundary count on
a patch and the coincidence count is a count on a torus; they are different quantities, no
theorem carries from one to the other, and there is no dynamics on the tiling for either to
govern. What the comparison does show is that the self-similarity of Chair44 produces the area-law
interface that any coarse-graining hierarchy does, and that the quantity which controls near-periodicity lies
one dimension below it.

\subsection{Periodic approximants: a tight-binding electron and the diffraction}
\label{sec:chair-approx}

A periodic approximant is the standard device for studying an aperiodic structure by finite means.
The label field $\sigma^M(j)$ on the $2^M$-cube, read as a torus $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$ of $8^M$ cells,
is one for Chair44. Take one site per unit cell. A cell with label $(f,k)$ (a rotation $f$
and a cell $k$ of the chair) at the point $p$ lies in the chair with translation $p-o_k(f)$, where
$o_k(f)$ is the position of the native cell $k$ under $f$; so two neighbouring cells lie in the same chair
exactly when their rotations agree and their native offsets differ by the lattice step. That test uses
the labels and the step only, so it is defined on a torus. Let the hopping be $-1$ inside a chair
and $-\lambda$ between chairs ($\lambda=\tfrac12$ here; the bounds below do not depend on the
weights). The label field is the tiling: $\sigma^3$ of the seven cells of a root chair reproduces
the labels of the level-$3$ patch, and the same-chair test reproduces the chair
partition of the patch, both checked.

On the torus of side $2^{M+1}$ there are two label fields: the true one, $\sigma^{M+1}(j)$, and the
\emph{lift} of the level-$M$ field, repeated $2\times2\times2$. Let $S_M$ be the set of sites where they differ.

\begin{proposition}[the approximants converge]
\label{prop:chair-approx}
Let $H_{M+1}$ be the Hamiltonian of the true field on the torus of side $2^{M+1}$ and $H_M^{\uparrow}$ that of the
lift. Then
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*), nosep]
\item $\lvert S_M\rvert\le8\,c(M)$, and in fact $\lvert S_M\rvert=\SSChSCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChSConst$ for $M\le\SSChApproxM{}$;
\item $\operatorname{rank}(H_{M+1}-H_M^{\uparrow})\le2\lvert S_M\rvert$, so the eigenvalue counting functions of the two differ by at most
  $2\lvert S_M\rvert$ at every energy, and their densities of states by at most $2c(M)/8^M\le\SSChIdsCoef\cdot4^{-M}$;
\item the spectrum of $H_M^{\uparrow}$ is the union of the spectra of the level-$M$ torus over the eight half-period twists
  (checked for $M\le\SSChDenseM{}$).
\end{enumerate}
In particular the integrated densities of states of the periodic approximants form a Cauchy sequence, uniformly in the energy,
at the geometric rate $4^{-M}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) The true field is the eight blocks $\sigma^M(\sigma(j)_b)$ and the lift is $\sigma^M(j)$ in every block. Two images of different labels
agree at every coincident address, so each block differs from the lift on at most $c(M)$ addresses. (b) A bond weight depends on
the labels of its two ends only, so $D=H_{M+1}-H_M^{\uparrow}$ vanishes on every bond with no end in $S_M$. With $P$ the projection onto
$S_M$, $D=PD+(1-P)DP$, of rank at most $2\lvert S_M\rvert$. For Hermitian matrices whose difference has rank $r$, the eigenvalue
counting functions differ by at most $r$ at every energy, by interlacing. Dividing by $8^{M+1}$ and using (a) gives
the density bound. Passing from the torus to the infinite periodic operator averages over the Bloch phases, and the same bound holds
phase by phase. (c) is a computation.
\end{proof}

\small

\toprule

\midrule

$8\,c(M)$ & \SSChSBoundRow \\
$\operatorname{rank}(H_{M+1}-H_M^{\uparrow})$ & \SSChRankRow \\
largest count difference & \SSChDNRow & --- & --- \\
as a fraction of the $8^{M+1}$ sites & \SSChDNFrac & --- & --- \\
proved bound, $2\lvert S_M\rvert/8^{M+1}$ & \SSChDNBound & --- & --- \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
\emph{Status.} The inequalities in (a) and (b) are proved. The equality in (a) is counted, and (c) is computed. The bound is loose: the observed difference is far below it, and
at $M\le2$ the bound exceeds one and says nothing. The observed rank is $\SSChRankCoef\cdot2^M$ for $M\le\SSChRankM{}$, well
under $2\lvert S_M\rvert$; that pattern is a finite check and not a theorem. The counts for $M\le\SSChDenseM{}$ come from dense
diagonalisation of tori of up to $\SSChDenseSites{}$ sites; the ranks, for $M\le\SSChRankM{}$, use only the support of $D$.

\paragraph{The six choices.} Start from the six labels $j$ with $\sigma(j)_{000}=\sigma(j)_{111}=j$ (the chair stack of~\cite{HartshornRomanov}).
They grow six label fields on the same $2^M$-cube. Any two differ on exactly the tube along the body diagonal $(1,1,1)$, which has
$7\cdot2^M-6$ cells, and on every cell of it all six labels are different (for $M\le\SSChSixM{}$). The ambiguity that the choice of $j$ leaves
in an approximant is therefore one tube, the same line defect, and by the argument of \cref{prop:chair-approx}(b) it moves the density of states by at most
$2(7\cdot2^M-6)/8^M$.

\paragraph{Diffraction.} Mark the cell of native index $0$ of each chair: a point set of density $1/7$. Let
$\hat\rho_M(m)=8^{-M}\sum_x\rho(x)e^{-2\pi i\,m\cdot x/2^M}$. At a wave vector $k=2\pi m/2^M$ of the level-$M$ torus the lift has the same coefficient
as the level-$M$ field, so $\lvert\hat\rho_{M+1}(k)-\hat\rho_M(k)\rvert\le\lvert S_M\rvert/8^{M+1}\le c(M)/8^M$, uniformly in $k$: every Bragg amplitude at a dyadic
wave vector has a limit, with that error (proved). The observed change falls by a factor of about $\SSChAmpFall$ per level, faster than the
bound's $4$, and is $\SSChAmpLast$ at $M=\SSChApproxM{}$ against the bound $\SSChAmpBoundLast$. The total intensity $\sum_m\lvert\hat\rho_M(m)\rvert^2$ is the density (Parseval); at $M=\SSChBraggM{}$ it is
$\SSChBraggTotal$, against $1/7=0.14286$, of which the zero wave vector carries $\SSChBraggZero\approx1/49$. The part on wave vectors with denominator dividing $2^{M_0}$ is
$\SSChBraggTwo$, $\SSChBraggThree$, $\SSChBraggFour$, $\SSChBraggFive$ for $M_0=2,3,4,5$, and the weight still missing from $1/7$ falls by a factor of about $\SSChBraggFall$ per level.
That is what pure-point diffraction with Bragg masses summing to the density looks like, and it is measured here, not proved: pure-point diffraction of
Chair44 is quoted from~\cite{HartshornRomanov}, through the theorem of \cite{LeeMoody,Schlottmann,LMS}.

\paragraph{A parallel, and where it stops.} Comparing an extensive quantity on a torus with the same quantity on its double is the
shape of the extensivity defect in the OpenAI preprint on the two-dimensional $\mathrm{O}(3)$ model~\cite{OAIO3}. There, for the
partition function $Z(n,w)$ on an $n\times w$ torus, $0\le4\log Z(n,w)-\log Z(2n,2w)$ is exponentially small in $\min(n,w)$
(its Proposition~2.1(ii)), and a doubling criterion (its Proposition~8.3) gives $\Delta(2^kn,2^kw)\le64^{-1}2^{-2^k}$ from a quadratic
recursion that uses only the positivity of a transfer operator. The comparison ends there. The bound above is geometric, not doubly exponential;
it comes from counting mismatched sites and not from a recursion; the difference of counting functions has no sign; and the system is a deterministic
tiling with no coupling to flow. Nothing in \cite{OAIO3} is used here and nothing transfers.

\paragraph{What this does not say.} The Hamiltonian is a model with arbitrary weights on a cubic lattice, not a description of any material, and $\lambda=\tfrac12$ was chosen
and not fitted. Nothing is claimed about gaps, localisation or the nature of the spectrum of the limiting operator, only that its
integrated density of states exists and is approached at a known rate. The six-sphere contains no Chair44, so none of this bears on it.

\subsection{The frame mismatch: a dihedral line defect}
\label{sec:chair-mismatch}

The descent defect of \cref{prop:chair-descent} counts cells whose labels differ from those of the translate. A label is a pair
(rotation, native cell), and the rotation $\operatorname{rot}(x)$ lies in the rotation group $O\cong S_4$ of the cube, the frame of
the chair that contains the cell. So translation by $2^Me$ carries a second, group-valued piece of information, the
\emph{frame mismatch}
\[
  \rho_e(x)=\operatorname{rot}(x+2^Me)\,\operatorname{rot}(x)^{-1}\in O .
\]
Because the label field is single valued on $\RR^3$, $\rho_e$ is the coboundary of the frame field, and satisfies the cocycle identity
$\rho_{e+e'}(x)=\rho_{e'}(x+2^Me)\,\rho_e(x)$. What it would take for this to be a connection on the torus $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$ is that it be a
function on the torus, one value per address; then it would be a flat $O$-bundle, with a class in $H^1(T_M;O)$ obstructing the descent. The
expectation that it is, and that it lives on the tubes, can be tested on the level-$N$ patch ($N=6$) for $M=\SSChMisFirst,\dots,\SSChMisLast$.

\begin{proposition}[the frame mismatch is a dihedral line defect]
\label{prop:chair-mismatch}
For $M=\SSChMisMs$ and each of the three axes $e$, on the level-$6$ patch:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*),leftmargin=*]
\item $\rho_e=1$ at every address where the label is determined, and wherever the labels of $x$ and $x+2^Me$ agree. So $\rho_e\ne1$ only on the four tubes
of \cref{prop:chair-tubes}.
\item At every six-label address of a tube along the body diagonal $\delta$, $\rho_e$ lies in the dihedral group of order $6$ that fixes $\delta$
(three half-turns and two third-turns, a copy of $S_3$, not abelian), and \emph{all six} of its elements occur there.
\item At the sixteen $24$-label addresses, the eight corners of the cube (where the tubes end) and the eight central cells (where all four meet),
all $24$ rotations occur: $\rho_e$ generates $O$.
\item $\rho_e$ is not a function on the torus: several values occur at one address. It defines no flat $O$-connection on $T_M$ and no
class in $H^1(T_M;O)$.
\item The cocycle identity holds on every cell where the three translates are present, and translation by $2^{M-1}e$ disagrees on a fraction
that lies between $c(M)/8^M$ and $c(M-1)/8^{M-1}$ (at $M=\SSChMisLast$: $\SSChMisHalf$, between $\SSChMisBound$ and $\SSChMisBoundPrev$), so the support belongs to the scale $2^M$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
(a) At a determined address the label is the same in every level-$M$ block, so labels, hence rotations, agree. (b): at a six-label address both labels lie in the same six-set, whose rotations form a coset of an order-six dihedral group about a body
diagonal (\cref{obs:chair-six}), so the quotient $ab^{-1}$ of two of them lies in a dihedral group of order six. That the diagonal is the tube's own, that all six
values occur, and (c), (d), (e) are counted on the patch, which is a genuine tiling patch, so every value found is a value that occurs.
\end{proof}

So the expected structure is half there. The mismatch is supported on the four tubes and takes values in a non-abelian group, the stabiliser of the tube's diagonal,
growing to all of $O$ at the sixteen end and junction cells. But it is not a flat connection of the torus. Over a point of $T_M$
the tiling offers one frame at a determined address, a torsor for the dihedral group (six frames) at a tube address, and a torsor for $O$ at sixteen
addresses, and the mismatch moves among all of them from one block to the next. On $\RR^3$ it is trivial cohomologically, being a coboundary; on
the torus it is not defined. The honest summary is a line defect with dihedral values, not an obstruction class.

\subsection{Two matches that are not connections}
\label{sec:chair-nolink}

\paragraph{A three-dimensional cusp analogue.} The $A_2$ triangulation behind the cusp
fan is Kuhn's triangulation of $\ZZ^2$. Its three-dimensional version, Kuhn's
triangulation of $\ZZ^3$, has $1,7,12,6$ simplices per fundamental domain ($\chi=0$; the
plane has $1,3,2$) and $24=4!$ simplices round a vertex, whose dual polytope, the
truncated octahedron, plays the role of the hexagon of the $dP_6$. The count $24$ is
also the order of the rotation group of the cube, the orientation group of Chair44, and
one would like it to be a symmetry. It is not: of the $\SSChKuhnGroup{}$ elements of the
cube group, $\SSChKuhnPreserve{}$ preserve the complex ($\SSChKuhnRot{}$ of the $24$
rotations); the complex has a linear automorphism group of order $\SSChKuhnAut{}$ meeting
the cube group in those $\SSChKuhnMeet{}$ elements; and the two groups are not conjugate
in $\GL(3,\ZZ)$, because reduced modulo two the cube group fixes a nonzero vector of
$\mathbb F_2^3$ and the other fixes none. The same mismatch is visible in the plane, where
the cusp torus is hexagonal and the square lattice is not. So $24=24$ is numerology, and
Chair44 does not sit equivariantly on this complex.

\paragraph{The orbifold group.} The rotation group of the cube is a quotient of the base
orbifold group $\mathbb Z/3*\mathbb Z/4$ of the construction, with the cusp loop sent to
a half-turn (the $(2,3,4)$ triangle group). The corresponding $S_4$ cover of the orbifold
is smooth, of genus $\SSChCoverGenus{}$ with $\SSChCoverCusps{}$ cusps
(Riemann--Hurwitz). This is exact group theory; nothing computed here shows that the
monodromy representation of the six-sphere factors through the quotient.

\subsection{What this says}

The Hat's substrate descends to $\TT$ and the Hat does not. Chair44's inflation is a
lattice map, so it has a whole tower of tori to nearly descend to, with a defect that is
exactly $c(M)/8^M\sim28\cdot4^{-M}$ (\cref{prop:chair-descent}). That is the finite
shadow of what the cited theory states in the limit: a primitive lattice substitution
with a modular coincidence has pure point, limit-periodic structure
\cite{LeeMoody,Schlottmann,LMS}, which we quote and do not re-prove. All
$\SSChChecks{}$ checks of \texttt{chair44\_nested.status} pass. The hull theorem is quoted
from~\cite{HartshornRomanov}, not re-derived here.

\section{Central Charges on Calabi--Yau Threefolds}
\label{sec:links}

We ask what the six-sphere's finite layer shares with three standard objects: a
reflexive polygon, the lattice $H^2(K3,\ZZ)$ with Kodaira's classification, and
Bridgeland central charges on Calabi--Yau threefolds. Each number below is
reached by at least two routes.

\subsection{The cusp component and the volume product}
\label{sec:links-mahler}

The component of the cusp fibre is the degree-six del Pezzo surface $dP_6$,
the toric surface whose polygon is $B_3$, the hexagon with \SSDPBoundary{}
boundary points. Its anticanonical degree $K^2$ is the normalised volume
$V(B_3) = 2\,\mathrm{Area}(B_3)$ (the identity $V(P) = (-K)^n$ for the
Gorenstein toric Fano variety whose anticanonical polytope is the reflexive
polytope $P$; $B_3$ is self-dual, so there is no ambiguity about which of the
pair is meant).

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:dp6}
$K^2_{dP_6} = \SSDPKTwo$. This is computed four ways, with no shared code:
by triangulating the hexagon ($V(B_3) = \SSDPVolume$); by Ehrhart counts of
the same polygon; by Noether's formula $K^2 = 12 - n = 12 - \SSDPRays$ for a
smooth complete toric surface with $n$ rays~\cite{Fulton}; and as $9 - 3$ for
$\mathbb{P}^2$ blown up at three points. The toric divisors have self-intersections $(-1)^{\times 6}$, and
\[
  \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} D_i^2}_{(-1)\times 6\,=\,-6}
  =\underbrace{12-3n}_{n=6}
  \qquad\text{while}\qquad
  K^2=\underbrace{12-n}_{n=6}=6 ,
\]
as they must.
\end{proposition}

The hexagon is self-dual: $V(B_3^*) = \SSDPDual$, so the volume product is
$V(B_3)V(B_3^*) = \SSDPProduct$. It is centrally symmetric, and the Mahler
bound for symmetric bodies is $4^2\cdot 2! = \SSDPBoundSym$, so $B_3$ lies
strictly above it (only two reflexive polygons attain it). The number that
the dictionary of \cref{sec:laves} reads off as six kites per domain, six
$(-1)$-curves of the hexagon, six corners, is therefore also the volume of
the polytope; the agreement is the toric fact that a smooth toric surface has
as many torus-fixed points as rays, together with $K^2 = 12 - n$, and it is
specific to $n = 6$. We claim nothing beyond that.

\subsection{The elliptic fibration and the lattice module}
\label{sec:links-lattice}

The middle block of the monodromy is that of an elliptic family over
$\mathbb{P}^1$ with singular fibres over the three points of the orbifold,
so each local monodromy fixes a Kodaira type up to the usual inversion: order
$3$ means $\mathrm{IV}$ or $\mathrm{IV}^*$, order $4$ means $\mathrm{III}$ or
$\mathrm{III}^*$, and the cusp, being unipotent, is of type
$\mathrm{I}_n$ ($n = 1$ here). The Euler numbers of the singular fibres of an elliptic
surface over $\mathbb{P}^1$ sum to $12\,\chi(\mathcal{O})$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kodaira}
For orders $(3,4)$ and an $\mathrm{I}_1$ cusp there are \SSKodairaCandidates{}
candidate triples, with Euler totals $\SSKodairaTotals$. Exactly one is a
multiple of $12$:
\[
  \SSKodairaTriple, \qquad
  \underbrace{8}_{e(\mathrm{IV}^*)} + \underbrace{3}_{e(\mathrm{III})}
  + \underbrace{1}_{e(\mathrm{I}_1)} = \underbrace{12}_{12\chi(\mathcal O)} .
\]
Its Jacobian is a rational elliptic surface; a K3 (total $24$) is impossible.
\end{proposition}

The same enumeration reproduces the two examples of \cref{sec:triangle},
$\mathrm{IV}^*+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{I}_2$ for $(3,6)$ and
$\mathrm{III}^*+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{I}_1$ for $(4,6)$, and so is checked
against the paper's own Euler arithmetic before being used for $(3,4)$. The
trivial lattice of such a surface is $U \oplus R$ with $R$ the root lattice
of the reducible fibres, and the Mordell--Weil rank is
$10 - 2 - \operatorname{rk}R$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:roots}
The root lattices $E_6 \oplus A_1$ and $E_7$ embed in $E_8$ both integrally
and over $\QQ$. Integrally: the $\SSERootsEight$ roots of $E_8$ are generated
from its Cartan matrix; the roots orthogonal to one root $\alpha$ form $E_7$
($\SSERootsSeven$ roots, rank $7$, discriminant \SSEDiscSeven{}); those
orthogonal to an $A_2 \ni \alpha$ form $E_6$ ($\SSERootsSix$ roots, rank
$6$, discriminant \SSEDiscSix{}), and $A_1 = \langle \alpha \rangle \subset
A_2$ is orthogonal to it, giving $E_6 \oplus A_1$ of discriminant
\SSEDiscSixA{}. Over $\QQ$ the Hasse--Minkowski test of
\texttt{pyCICY.k3lattice} accepts both. A control fails as it should:
$E_7 \oplus \langle 3 \rangle$ has the right rank but the wrong
discriminant and does not embed. In all three configurations ($\mathrm{IV}^*
+ \mathrm{III} + \mathrm{I}_1$, $\mathrm{IV}^* + \mathrm{II} + \mathrm{I}_2$,
$\mathrm{III}^* + \mathrm{II} + \mathrm{I}_1$) the Mordell--Weil rank is
$\SSMWRank$.
\end{proposition}

So the paper's $(3,4,\infty)$ data and its $(3,6,\infty)$ example have the
same root part, $E_6 \oplus A_1$, although the fibres differ. The scope is
narrow. Euler counting shows that the local data is consistent with a
rational elliptic surface of that configuration; it does not show that the
six-sphere's family \emph{is} such a surface, and whether the configuration
exists is a question about the Miranda--Persson classification~\cite{MP86}
that we do not decide. The $\QQ$-embedding is a necessary condition only;
the integral statement is the explicit construction inside $E_8$.

\subsection{Where the central-charge module stops}
\label{sec:links-cc}

The central-charge module computes the charges $Z(E) = -\int e^{-B-i\omega}
\mathrm{ch}(E)$ (ordinary and square-root-Todd) of the large-volume
stability conditions announced for smooth projective threefolds with trivial
canonical bundle. They need a real class $\omega$ with $\omega^3 > 0$. The
six-sphere has $H^2(X,\mathbb{R}) = 0$, so it carries no such class: it is
not K\"ahler, and the module's own guard \SSGuardRefused{} the empty class. We record this as a result rather than a gap. The
finite layer behind the six-sphere is rigid, but the charges that give the
CICY side its stability-theoretic content have no counterpart here.

\section{Curves on Surfaces: the Dual Complex as a Punctured Torus}
\label{sec:surface}

The curve formalism of Arkani-Hamed, Frost, Salvatori, Plamondon and Thomas~\cite{AHFPT} writes scattering data as counts of curves
on a surface, and the leading singularities of Carr\^olo and Figueiredo~\cite{CF} are such counts on a fatgraph. The cluster algebras of
surfaces of Fomin, Shapiro and Thurston~\cite{FST} give the algebra behind both. This section asks what the
dual complex of $W$ is as a surface in that language, and what in the six-sphere's integer data meets the $n$-gon leading singularity.
It is computed by \texttt{pyCICY.sixsphere\_surface}, which uses the \texttt{pyCICY.theories} module for the $n$-gon.

\subsection{The dual complex is the Markov triangulation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:surf-markov}
Read as an ideal triangulation of the once-punctured torus, $\Delta(W)$ (\cref{prop:dualcx}) has the Fomin--Shapiro--Thurston quiver with
three vertices $D_a,D_b,D_c$ and a double arrow between each pair, the Markov quiver
\begin{equation}
  B=\begin{pmatrix}0&2&-2\\-2&0&2\\2&-2&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad x_a\,x_a'=x_b^2+x_c^2 ,
  \label{eq:markov-quiver}
\end{equation}
and cyclically for $b,c$. Mutation preserves $(x_a^2+x_b^2+x_c^2)/(x_ax_bx_c)$; from $(1,1,1)$ it produces the Markov triples
\SSSfTriples, \dots, all with $x^2+y^2+z^2=3xyz$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Computation]
The cyclic order of sides round each triangle is read from the counter-clockwise vertices of the two $A_2$ triangles: $(D_a,D_b,D_c)$ for $P$ and
$(D_c,D_a,D_b)$ for $Q$, the same cyclic order. Each triangle contributes the $3$-cycle $a\to b\to c\to a$, so each pair of arcs gets two arrows,
$\SSSfArrows$ in all, which is \eqref{eq:markov-quiver}. The exchange relation is the mutation rule, and the invariant is checked on every one of the
$\SSSfSeeds$ seeds within $\SSSfDepth$ mutations. As a control, reversing the orientation of one triangle makes the arrows cancel and the matrix
is not Markov. Every one of the $\SSSfVariables$ cluster variables met is a Laurent polynomial with positive coefficients, which is a theorem
for cluster algebras of surfaces~\cite{FST,MSW} and is used here as a check on the code and not as a result.
\end{proof}

The three double curves are therefore the three arcs of the Markov triangulation, and the middle blocks of $T_1,T_2$ (\cref{sec:lucas-trace}) generate
$\SL_2(\ZZ)$, the mapping class group of the once-punctured torus. Whether this surface carries an amplitude is settled in \cref{sec:surf-loops}: it does not.

\paragraph{A number that looks like a match.} Six oriented edges of $\Delta(W)$ (\cref{sec:dual}) and three arcs with two tags each (the tagged arcs of~\cite{FST}) are both
$3\times2$. They are different involutions. Orientation reversal of a double curve is the central element $-I\in\SL_2(\ZZ)$, which fixes every arc, and
the tag is a label that $-I$ also fixes. We argue this and do not compute it, and we record it as a count that two things share, not as an identification.

\subsection{Two fans: a hexagon and a line}

Both the Mumford fan of the cusp (cones over the triangles of the $A_2$ triangulation at height $1$) and the $g$-vector fan of the Markov
cluster algebra are fans of unimodular cones. For the second this is automatic, since the $g$-vectors of a cluster form a $\ZZ$-basis, so it is not evidence of a link.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:surf-fans}
Every ray of the Mumford fan lies in exactly $\SSSfHexCones$ maximal cones, a hexagon. In the Markov cluster complex the clusters through one variable form an
infinite line: within $\SSSfLinkDepth$ mutations of a seed there are $\SSSfLinkClusters$ clusters, and $\SSSfLinkThrough=2\cdot\SSSfLinkDepth+1$ of them contain the
variable $x_a$, as a line gives and a $6$-cycle cannot. The two fans are not isomorphic.
\end{proposition}

The count is a finite-depth computation consistent with the line; that the link is infinite is the known structure of the once-punctured torus (cutting along an arc leaves an annulus, whose triangulations are indexed by $\ZZ$).
The six-sphere's fan is periodic and its link is finite, so the match with the surface fan stops at the incidence data of \cref{prop:surf-markov}.

\subsection{Loop order: a vacuum diagram, not an amplitude}
\label{sec:surf-loops}

In the curve formalism a fatgraph with $V$ cubic vertices, $E$ edges and $F$ boundary cycles on a surface of genus $g$ has $L=E-V+1=2g+F-1$ loops, and
external states are marked points on a boundary. The dual of $\Delta(W)$ has a vertex for each triangle and an edge for each arc, so it is the theta graph, with
the cyclic order of arcs at $P$ and at $Q$ read from \cref{prop:dualcx}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:surf-loops}
The dual fatgraph of $\Delta(W)$ has $V=2$, $E=3$, $F=\SSSfFaces$ boundary cycle, genus $\SSSfGenus$ and $L=\SSSfLoops$ loops by both formulas, and no external legs. It is the
genus-one embedding of the two-loop theta (sunrise) vacuum diagram. Its Symanzik polynomial is $U=\alpha_a\alpha_b+\alpha_b\alpha_c+\alpha_c\alpha_a$, from the matrix-tree theorem and from the
$\SSSfTrees$ spanning trees, and its Newton polytope is a unimodular triangle.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Computation]
The boundary cycles are the orbits of the face permutation of the rotation system $(a,b,c)$ at $P$, $(c,a,b)=(a,b,c)$ at $Q$: one orbit. Then $2-2g=V-E+F$ gives $g=\SSSfGenus$, and
$E-V+1=2g+F-1=\SSSfLoops$. The control, the opposite cyclic order at $Q$, has $3$ faces and genus $0$ and is the planar theta graph. $U$ is computed by the two routes of
\texttt{theories.softgraph}.
\end{proof}

The surface has no boundary and therefore no marked point on one: it carries no external state, so the once-punctured torus is a two-loop vacuum diagram and not a scattering
amplitude. $U$ does not see the embedding, so the planar and the genus-one theta graph have the same $U$; the genus enters only through the faces. The symmetry of $U$ in
$\alpha_a,\alpha_b,\alpha_c$ is the permutation of the three double curves.

\subsection{F-polynomials and slopes on the torus}

With principal coefficients the exchange relation of the extended Markov quiver is $x_kx_k'=\prod\tilde x_i^{[\tilde b_{ik}]_+}+\prod\tilde x_i^{[-\tilde b_{ik}]_+}$, the frozen variables being
$y_0,y_1,y_2$ and the matrix $\tilde B$ mutating along with the cluster.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:surf-F}
For all $\SSSfVariables$ cluster variables within $\SSSfDepth$ mutations of $\Delta(W)$:
(i) each is homogeneous for $\deg x_i=e_i$, $\deg y_j=-b_j$, with $g$-vector its degree;
(ii) $F=X|_{x=1}$ has constant term $1$, positive coefficients and a unique maximal monomial divisible by all others;
(iii) $X=x^{g}F(\hat y)$ with $\hat y_j=y_j\prod_ix_i^{b_{ij}}$, a second route to the same variable;
(iv) the $g$-vectors of every cluster form a $\ZZ$-basis;
(v) assigning to each arc the slope $p/q$ of $D=pe_1+qe_2$ in $\Lam$, with $D_a,D_b,D_c=(1,0),(-1,1),(0,1)$ and mutation replacing $v_k$ by the other of $v_i\pm v_j$, is a bijection
from these variables to slopes; arcs in a cluster have $|\det|=1$ and the $\SSSfPExchange$ exchange pairs have $|\det|=2$, so the arcs cross $|\det|-1$ times.
\end{proposition}

(i)--(iv) are known~\cite{FST,MSW}, and are here as checks of the code against two routes. (v) says that the cluster complex is the Farey complex, as the Markov numbers show. It also shows what is not
finite on this surface. Every arc is crossed once by infinitely many arcs: for the arc $(1,0)$ these are the $(r,2)$ with $r$ odd, of which there are $\SSSfCrossTen$ with $|r|\le10$ and $\SSSfCrossTwenty$ with $|r|\le20$.
A $u$-equation for an arc, $u_C+\prod_{C'}u_{C'}^{\#(C,C')}=1$, is therefore an infinite product on this surface, with infinitely many factors even at a fixed crossing number.

\subsection{The u-variables from the F-polynomials, and the puncture}
\label{sec:surf-u}

The disk module defines $u_{ij}$ as a cross-ratio of $F$-polynomials. We do not use a formula. With principal coefficients put $y_i=t^{m_i}$ and let $t\to0$; the
$u$-variables of binary geometry vanish there like $t^{\nu_\gamma(m)}$, where $\nu_\gamma(m)$ is the coordinate of $m$ on the ray $-g_\gamma$ of the $g$-fan cone containing $m$,
and $0$ off the cone. Requiring $\nu_\gamma$ to be a combination of the valuations $\min_{\mathrm{mon}\in F_h}\langle\mathrm{mon},m\rangle$ of the $F$-polynomials and of the linear forms $\langle\mu,m\rangle$
is an integer linear problem, whose solution is $u_\gamma=y^{\mu}\prod_hF_h^{E_{\gamma h}}$. The exponents of the $u$-equations
$u_\gamma+\prod_{h}u_h^{C_{\gamma h}}=1$ are then found independently, by least squares on $\log(1-u_\gamma)$ at random rational points and rounding, and every equation is
verified exactly at twelve fresh rational points.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:surf-u}
In type $A_2$, $A_3$ and in type $D_4$ (the once-punctured square, whose oriented $4$-cycle is the fan triangulation, with $\SSSfUDVars$ cluster variables in $\SSSfUDClusters$ clusters)
the solution is unique, integral and local: each $u_\gamma$ has at most four $F$-factors, with exponents $\pm1$. The $u$-equations hold exactly, $C$ is symmetric, and $C_{\gamma h}=0$ exactly when
$\gamma,h$ lie in a common cluster; its values are $\{0,1\}$ in type $A$ and $\{0,1,2\}$ in type $D_4$. For the hexagon the $\SSSfUHexVars$ values equal those of the disk module
after reversing the order of the three $y$'s. In $D_4$, sorting the $12$ non-initial variables by their $d$-vector (the row of $C$ on the initial variables) gives $4$ notched arcs $N_j$
(zero at $j$, ones elsewhere) and $\SSSfUDChords$ chords. Writing $P_i$ for the plain arc to the puncture and $c_{a,b}$ for the chord with $d$-vector supported on $\{a,b\}$,
\begin{equation}
  u(P_i)=\frac{F(c_{i+2,i+3})}{F(N_{i+1})},\qquad u(N_j)=y_{j+1}\frac{F(c_{j+2,j+3})}{F(N_j)},\qquad i,j\in\ZZ/4 .
  \label{eq:D4-u}
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}[Computation]
The regression, the exact check and the control are as described. As a control, solving with $+g$ instead of $-g$ leaves variables of $A_2$ without a solution. The names $P_i$, $N_j$ and $c_{a,b}$
are read from the $d$-vectors by the crossing-number rule of~\cite{FST}, and are not checked geometrically here.
\end{proof}

So the arc to the puncture has, in $u$, its endpoint moved by one step and its tag flipped, as the cross-ratio picture of spiral endpoints would have it: a spiral at the puncture is a tagged arc, and no formula beyond the cluster algebra is needed in
finite type.
This holds for the punctured disk and says nothing yet about the torus.

\paragraph{The torus.} The Markov quiver has no complete $g$-fan: the cones of the clusters cover only part of $\RR^3$. Two readings of the same ansatz fail. Demanding $\nu=0$ off the fan, as holds in finite type where there is no ``off'', the initial arcs
have no solution at depth $\SSSfUTorusDepthA$ or $\SSSfUTorusDepthB$ (of $\SSSfUTorusVarsA$ and $\SSSfUTorusVarsB$ variables, $\SSSfUTorusUnsolvedA$ and $\SSSfUTorusUnsolvedB$ have no integral solution, which includes variables at the frontier of the truncation).
Fitting on the fan alone, the system has rank $\SSSfUTorusRankB$ for $\SSSfUTorusUnkB$ unknowns at depth $\SSSfUTorusDepthB$, so the fit is not unique. So there is no canonical $u_\gamma$ of this form on the torus, and we do not define one.
We suspect, and have not shown, that this is where the infinite products of \cref{prop:surf-F} enter, through the part of $\RR^3$ outside the fan. The $u$-equations on the torus are not checked. That is a statement about this ansatz, truncated at depth $\SSSfUTorusDepthB$; a $u$
built from the spirals of the surface, which we do not have, might exist.

\subsection{Lucas as traces of powers}
\label{sec:lucas-trace}

The leading singularity of the $n$-gon in the limit $X_{i,j}\to X$, $X_{i,p}\to Y$ is $(2-D)Y^n-\sum_kC_kY^kX^{n-k}$ with $C_k=[x^{n-2k}]L_n(x)$ the Lucas
coefficients, a recurrence the source papers do not explain.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:surf-lucas}
Let $a_\pm$ be the roots of $a^2-Xa+XY$. Then $\sum_kC_kY^kX^{n-k}=a_+^n+a_-^n=\operatorname{tr}\begin{pmatrix}X&-XY\\1&0\end{pmatrix}^n$, so the
leading singularity is $(2-D)Y^n-(a_+^n+a_-^n)$. In terms of $x=\sqrt{X/Y}$, the matrix $M/\sqrt{XY}$ has determinant $1$ and trace $x$,
and $\operatorname{tr}(M/\sqrt{XY})^n=L_n(x)$. For the middle blocks of the monodromy,
\begin{equation}
  A=\begin{pmatrix}-1&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad B=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\quad AB=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\0&1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  \operatorname{tr}A^n=L_n(-1),\ \operatorname{tr}B^n=L_n(0),\ \operatorname{tr}(AB)^n=L_n(2),
  \label{eq:lucas-blocks}
\end{equation}
of periods $3$, $4$ and constant $2$, on the loci $X=Y$, $X=0$ and $X=4Y$ of the kinematics, the last being where the two roots coincide.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The power sums $p_n=a_+^n+a_-^n$ satisfy $p_n=Xp_{n-1}-XYp_{n-2}$ with $p_0=2$, $p_1=X$ (Cayley--Hamilton for the companion matrix), which is the Lucas
recurrence after the scaling $x=\sqrt{X/Y}$, $p_n=(XY)^{n/2}L_n(x)$; expanding $L_n$ gives the coefficients $C_k$. The identity with the
module's \texttt{ngon\_leading\_singularity} is checked symbolically for $n=2,\dots,\SSSfLucasN$, and the traces in \eqref{eq:lucas-blocks} for $n\le\SSSfLucasN$.
The middle blocks are read from $T_1,T_2$ (\cref{sec:flag}).
\end{proof}

This answers the open question of the Lucas recurrence as algebra: the numbers are the traces of the powers of one $2\times2$ matrix. It does not say why the
$n$-gon leading singularity is governed by that matrix, and it is not a physical link between the six-sphere and the amplitude. The values $x=-1,0,2$ are three of the five integer
values with $|x|\le2$, the traces of the elliptic and parabolic elements of $\SL_2(\ZZ)$.

\paragraph{The Hat is the same identity at $x=\SSSfHatX$.} The Hat's substitution matrix has characteristic polynomial $(\lambda-1)(\lambda+1)(\lambda^2-7\lambda+1)$, so
$\operatorname{tr}M^n=L_{4n}+1+(-1)^n$ for $n\le8$ and its Perron root $\varphi^4$ is the hyperbolic case $x=\SSSfHatX=L_4$. The six-sphere monodromy is elliptic and parabolic, the
Hat's inflation is hyperbolic, and both are the one Cayley--Hamilton identity. This is the quadratic-unit match of \cref{sec:cosmo-nolink} again, and it is not a connection.

\subsection{What this does and does not say}

The cusp fibre's dual complex is exactly the Markov triangulation, its monodromy blocks generate the mapping class group of the surface, and the Lucas recurrence
of the $n$-gon is the trace of powers of a $2\times2$ matrix, with the $(3,4,\infty)$ data at $x=-1,0,2$. The surface is a two-loop genus-one vacuum diagram with no external state (\cref{prop:surf-loops}), and its $F$-polynomials and slopes are computed (\cref{prop:surf-F}). The $u$-variables are obtained from the $F$-polynomials in finite type, including the punctured disk with its tagged arcs (\cref{prop:surf-u}), and not on the torus. Not shown: the $u$-equations on the torus, that the
fan of the six-sphere is a fan of the cluster algebra (it is not, \cref{prop:surf-fans}), or that tagged arcs correspond to oriented edges. The Symanzik polynomials of the
toroidal quotient of the Laves graph were not computed.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The cusp fibre of Alpöge's six-sphere is the aperiodic monotile's substrate, at
the monotile's own distinguished point, rolled up on a torus
(\cref{thm:laves}). Folding a computed Hat tiling onto that torus, the tiling does
not descend, as it cannot: every one of the twelve bond orbits is sometimes a tile
boundary and sometimes not (\cref{prop:descent}, \cref{sec:rollup}). The mystic
tile, however, lands on exactly six points of the torus at every level from the
third on, one per orientation, each anchored over a double curve of $W$ and forming a
single orbit of the hexagon's rotation group (\cref{prop:orbit}); six is the number of
oriented edges of the dual complex $\Delta(W)$ (\cref{prop:dualcx}). The count is
proved for all levels by an exact finite automaton (\cref{thm:six}, computer-assisted),
and the Hat itself has six classes as well; whether six oriented double curves is
natural or a coincidence of the Hat point is open, and the dependence of surroundings
on class rests on small samples.

The Hat does not descend to $\TT$ because its inflation is irrational. Chair44, whose
inflation is the integer $2$, nearly descends to every torus $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$ of a nested
tower, with a defect bounded by $c(M)/8^M\sim28\cdot4^{-M}$ that is proved from a finite
recurrence certificate and measured on a patch of $\SSChPatchCells$ cells
(\cref{prop:chair-rate,prop:chair-descent}). That defect is a line: the undetermined addresses are four tubes along
the body diagonals (\cref{prop:chair-tubes}), while the interface between supertiles carries an area term
(\cref{prop:chair-interface}), and the transfer matrix of the coincidence count has no root that could give one. The group-valued frame mismatch
under the translation lives on that line with dihedral values, and all of the rotation group at its ends and centre, but is not a function on the torus
(\cref{prop:chair-mismatch}). On the
periodic approximants the same count bounds how far the density of states of a tight-binding model, and each Bragg
amplitude at a dyadic wave vector, move from one level to the next (\cref{prop:chair-approx}). That is a contrast of kind. The six-sphere
contains no Chair44, and two matches with it, the $24$ simplices round a vertex of the
Kuhn triangulation of $\ZZ^3$ and the $(3,4,\infty)$ group against the cube's rotation
group, are coincidences (\cref{sec:chair-nolink}). Two matches with cosmological singularities are of the same kind: the
Spectre's inflation and perimeter growth are closed orbits of the Mixmaster map, as every quadratic unit is, and the
pancake's fibre clock misses the six of $\mathbb Z/6$ (\cref{sec:cosmo-nolink}).

Read as an ideal triangulation of the once-punctured torus, $\Delta(W)$ has the Markov quiver, and the monodromy blocks generate
the mapping class group of that torus (\cref{prop:surf-markov}). The dual fatgraph is the genus-one two-loop theta vacuum diagram with no external state, so there is no amplitude
(\cref{prop:surf-loops}). In finite type, including the punctured disk, the $u$-variables follow from the $F$-polynomials with the $u$-equations exact and the tagged
arcs flipping their tag (\cref{prop:surf-u}); on the torus the same ansatz has no canonical solution. The Lucas recurrence of the $n$-gon leading singularity is the trace of powers of a $2\times2$ matrix, whose values at
the $(3,4,\infty)$ data are $x=-1,0,2$, while the Hat's inflation is the same identity at $x=\SSSfHatX$ (\cref{prop:surf-lucas}). The fan of the cusp is a hexagon at
every ray and the cluster complex a line, so the two fans differ (\cref{prop:surf-fans}).

Read as a gauge theory without principal bundles, the fibre carries a symplectic lattice with
a marked vector, and the group of its automorphisms is the integral Jacobi group; the
holonomy $\langle T_1,T_2\rangle$ is the kernel of a character onto $\ZZ/12$ (\cref{thm:gauge-index}), so it misses
exactly the central translations $\delta\mapsto\delta+k\gamma$ with $12\nmid k$, is Zariski dense in the real
group (\cref{prop:gauge-zariski}), and the four near-miss classes are the four divisibilities of
$\gamma$ (\cref{obs:gauge-classes}).

The character is the multiplier of $e(\beta/12)\theta_1(\tau,\mu)/\eta(\tau)$ (\cref{thm:theta}), so the holonomy
is the exact invariance group of that function on the laws.

A tight-binding electron on the cusp fibre's substrate has exact bands in the invariants of
$A_2$ (\cref{prop:ho-bands}), a magnetic field that restricts to the Hat as a connection with flux period $\tfrac12$ against $1$
(\cref{prop:ho-period}), and Chern numbers reproduced by a Bloch-bundle computation and by a real-space formula
(\cref{tab:ho-chern}); the Hat's own gaps are a measurement on one patch.

The lattice data was forced: within the flag class the triangle group by proof, the
matrices up to conjugacy in every range searched, with finiteness of the search
space the one step left unproved. The lattice is achiral and not self-dual over
$\ZZ$, its arithmetic meets the monotiles' in their geometry and not in their
inflation, and two resemblances with the Spectre are coincidences.

None of this bears on whether the construction is correct; that rests on analysis
and topology and on the external formalization~\cite{Alexeev}. The six-sphere
knows nothing of monotiles, and we draw no physical conclusion: the substrate
identity, the six fibres and the contacts of \cref{sec:links} are identities of
integers and of finite patches.
