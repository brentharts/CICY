\title{
The Aperiodic Einstein Monotile on the Cusp Torus of Alpöge's Complex Six-Sphere
}
\author{Brent S. Hartshorn \orcidlink{0009-0004-2853-655X} \small
\url{https://github.com/brentharts/CICY}}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The cusp fibre $W$ of Alpöge's complex structure on the six-sphere is the
$[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling rolled up on a torus $\TT = \RR^2/\Lam$,
$\Lam = B_0\ZZ^2$, and at the proportions of the Hat that tiling is exactly the
substrate of the aperiodic monotile: hexagon centres are components, edge
midpoints double curves, hexagon vertices triple points. No monotile tiling
descends to $\TT$, since an aperiodic tiling is invariant under no lattice. We
fold a Hat tiling generated by substitution onto $\TT$ anyway. Every tile is a
union of whole kites, so the fold is well defined, and we compute its fibres.
The ``mystic'' tile (the Turtle, ten kites) lands on exactly six points of
the torus in the patches of three and four substitution steps: one per
orientation, each anchored on the midpoint of a double curve of $W$, two
orientations per curve. The six classes form one orbit of the rotation group of
a hexagon, and six is the number of oriented edges of the dual complex of $W$,
which we show is the torus with one vertex, three edges and two triangles. We
prove that at most $72$ classes are possible, state six as a conjecture, and
show by the boundary statistics of the folded tiling that equal fibres do not
mean equal surroundings. The Hat's inflation is irrational, so it leaves no lattices to descend to.
Tsiokos's Chair44, a three-dimensional einstein tile, inflates by the integer $2$, and
its supertile lattices $2^M\ZZ^3$ give a tower of nested tori $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$. For this
tower we prove that a patch made of whole level-$M$ blocks agrees with its translate by
$2^Mv$ on at least a fraction $1-c(M)/8^M$ of its cells, where
$c(M)=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst$ counts the addresses at which the substitution's
modular coincidence has not yet occurred (a finite recurrence certificate), and we measure
the agreement on a patch of $\SSChPatchCells$ cells: $\SSChAgreeThree$, $\SSChAgreeFour$ and
$\SSChAgreeFive$ at $M=3,4,5$. The contrast with the Hat is one of kind and not an identity:
the six-sphere contains no Chair44, and two tempting matches with it, the $24$ simplices
round a vertex of the Kuhn triangulation of $\ZZ^3$ and the $(3,4,\infty)$ group against
the rotation group of the cube, are shown to be coincidences.
The paper also records why the lattice data
behind the construction is forced, that its monodromy is achiral and its
lattice not self-dual over $\ZZ$, and three exact contacts with standard
objects (a polytope volume, a Kodaira configuration, and central charges).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

In 1948 Hopf asked whether the six-sphere admits a complex
structure~\cite{Hopf48}. In August 2026 Alpöge posted a construction
answering yes~\cite{Alpoge}: a compact complex threefold $X$, fibred over
$\mathbb{P}^1$ by complex 2-tori, with a cusp fibre $W$ and two multiple
fibres over three points, proved diffeomorphic to $S^6$. Engel gave a
pedagogical account~\cite{Engel}, and Alexeev formalised the headline
statement in Lean~4~\cite{Alexeev}. The construction contradicts a published
corollary of Campana, Demailly and Peternell~\cite{CDP20}, and Alpöge locates
where the two accounts part. At the time of writing the result is not
refereed.

Nearly all of the proof is analysis and topology. It stands, however, on two
$4\times4$ integer matrices $T_1,T_2$ of orders $3$ and $4$ whose product is
unipotent, and much of what is proved is decided by linear algebra over $\ZZ$.
We work only with that finite layer, exactly, and ask what it is connected to.
The answer we pursue is the aperiodic monotile.

\paragraph{The substrate.} The cusp fibre $W$ is a degree-six del Pezzo surface
glued to itself, and its strata have the incidence structure of the $A_2$
triangulation of the plane modulo $\Lam$. The substrate of the Hat, the kite
graph of Smith, Myers, Kaplan and Goodman-Strauss~\cite{SmithHat}, is at the
Hat's own proportions the $[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling, and \cref{thm:laves} shows
that tiling is the Hasse diagram of that triangulation (\cref{fig:dictionary}).
The cusp fibre is the substrate \emph{rolled up}.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.7\textwidth]{ss_dictionary.pdf}
\caption{The dictionary at the Hat point (\cref{thm:laves}). (a) The cells of
the $A_2$ triangulation: vertices (circles), edge midpoints (squares), triangle
centroids (triangles). (b) The Laves substrate on the same sites; its bonds are
the cover relations vertex $<$ edge $<$ triangle. The shaded hexagon has one
centre, six edge midpoints and six vertices.}
\label{fig:dictionary}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Folding a tiling.} A periodic tiling can be rolled up and an
aperiodic one cannot, so the substrate descends and the tiling does not. That
concerns whole tilings. A finite patch of a Hat tiling is a union of tiles, each
a union of whole kites, and the quotient $\pi:\RR^2\to\TT$ sends it to a finite
multiset on the torus. \Cref{sec:rollup} does this for a substitution patch and
records, for each of the twelve bond orbits of $\TT$, how often the bond is a
tile boundary (\cref{fig:rollup}); none is always or never, which is the
certificate of non-descent.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{ss_hat_rollup.pdf}
\caption{A Hat tiling rolled up on the cusp torus (\cref{sec:rollup}).
(a) A substitution patch on the Laves lattice, mystic tiles marked.
(b) The torus, each bond orbit weighted by how often that bond is a tile
boundary. (c) The twelve boundary fractions $f(o)$, all strictly between $0$
and $1$.}
\label{fig:rollup}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Mystic fibres.} We follow the mystic, the ten-kite tile, through the
fold. Two mystics are in the same \emph{fibre class} if they differ by an
element of $\Lam$. In the three-step patch (443 tiles, 51 mystic) the mystics
form exactly six fibre classes, the same six occur at four steps, and five of
them at two steps. The classes are the six orientations of the mystic; in each,
a distinguished vertex lies over the midpoint of one of the three double curves
$D_a,D_b,D_c$, and orientations differing by $180^\circ$ share a double curve
(\cref{fig:n3}). The six classes are one orbit of the rotation group of a
hexagon, and six is the number of oriented edges of the dual complex of $W$
(\cref{sec:dual,sec:mystic}). We prove at most $72$ classes and conjecture
six (\cref{prop:bound,conj:six}); the dependence of the surroundings on the
class is observed, not proved.

\caption{Mystic tiles after three substitution steps (443 tiles, 51 mystic).
(a) One mystic, flat: its ten kites coloured by the torus kite they fold onto,

\label{fig:n3}

\paragraph{An integer inflation.} The Hat does not descend because its inflation factor is
irrational and its tiling is aperiodic, so no lattice is left invariant. Tsiokos's
Chair44~\cite{Tsiokos1}, a three-dimensional einstein tile, is aperiodic with the
\emph{integer} inflation $2$, so its level-$M$ supertiles sit on the nested lattices
$2^M\ZZ^3$ and the tiling is exhausted by the tori $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$. \Cref{sec:chair44}
proves that a patch of whole level-$M$ blocks agrees with its translate by $2^Mv$ on at
least $1-c(M)/8^M$ of its cells, with $c(M)=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst$, measures it on a
patch of $\SSChPatchCells$ cells (agreement $\SSChAgreeThree$, $\SSChAgreeFour$,
$\SSChAgreeFive$ at $M=3,4,5$, against the Hat's best $0.796$ on a different footing), and
settles two tempting matches with the six-sphere as coincidences. It is a contrast of kind:
the six-sphere contains no Chair44.

\section{The cusp fibre is the monotile's substrate}
\label{sec:laves}

\subsection{The substrate at the Hat point}

The Hat of~\cite{SmithHat,SmithSpectre} is a union of eight kites
(\cref{fig:hat}). A kite has legs $a$ from a hexagon centre to an edge midpoint
and $b$ from that edge midpoint to a hexagon vertex, meeting at right angles at
the edge midpoint, along directions that are multiples of $30^\circ$;
coordinates lie in $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ and are exact. Around a hexagon centre the six
edge midpoints are $a\,e_k$, $e_k = u_{30^\circ+60^\circ k}$, and each carries
two outer kite corners $a\,e_k \pm b\,e_k^{\perp}$, twelve per hexagon. Adjacent
corners coincide exactly when
\begin{equation}
  \overbrace{a\,e_k + b\,e_k^{\perp}}^{\text{corner of kite }k}
  \;=\;
  \overbrace{a\,e_{k+1} - b\,e_{k+1}^{\perp}}^{\text{corner of kite }k+1},
  \qquad\text{i.e.}\qquad
  a=\tfrac a2+\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}b
  \ \text{ and }\
  b = \tfrac{\sqrt3}{2}a-\tfrac b2
  \ \Longleftrightarrow\ a=\sqrt3\,b .
  \label{eq:merge}
\end{equation}
So at the Hat's proportions $a:b=\sqrt3:1$, and at no others, the twelve corners
merge in pairs into the six vertices of the $[3.4.6.4]$ Laves tiling. With
$a=\sqrt3$, $b=1$ the kite has area $\underbrace{2}_{\text{two triangles}}
\cdot\tfrac12 ab=\sqrt3$, and the Hat, eight kites, has area $8\sqrt3$.

\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{ss_hat_kites.pdf}
\caption{The Hat at $a:b=\sqrt3:1$ as eight whole kites of the $[3.4.6.4]$ Laves
lattice. Circles are hexagon centres (valence 6), squares edge midpoints
(valence 4), triangles hexagon vertices (valence 3). The thirteen-sided outline
has area $8\sqrt3$ in units where $b=1$.}
\label{fig:hat}
\end{figure}

On the six-sphere's side, the cusp fibre is built from a toric fan whose
cross-section is the $A_2$ triangulation of the plane~\cite{Alpoge}: its
vertices give the components of $W$, its edges the double curves, its triangles
the triple points, and the quotient by $B_0\ZZ^2$ identifies them.

\begin{theorem}
\label{thm:laves}
At the Hat point $a:b=\sqrt3:1$ the monotile substrate is the Hasse diagram of
the $A_2$ triangulation: its sites are the cells (hexagon centres the vertices,
edge midpoints the edges, hexagon vertices the triangles) and its bonds are
exactly the cover relations vertex $<$ edge $<$ triangle. At no other
proportions is it.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Computation]
Embed $A_2$ by $e_1\mapsto 2a\,u_{30^\circ}$, $e_2\mapsto 2a\,u_{90^\circ}$ with
vertices at hexagon centres; edge midpoints and triangle centroids follow
(\cref{fig:dictionary}). For the two-ring patch at the Hat point all
\SSLavesSites{} sites coincide exactly, in $\QQ(\sqrt3)$, with cells of the
matching kind, and the \SSLavesIncidences{} cover relations among them are
exactly its \SSLavesBonds{} bonds. That the corners merge at no other ratio is
\eqref{eq:merge}. At the Spectre's proportions $a=b$, and at $17/10:1$ (within
$2\%$ of $\sqrt3$), the corners stay apart: the patch has \SSSpectreSites{} and
\SSNearHatSites{} sites, its ``vertex'' sites have valence
\SSSpectreVertexValence{} rather than $3$, and the graph is a different graph,
not a metric distortion of the same one. The substrate is periodic and the patch
contains a fundamental domain with every cell incident to it, so agreement on
the patch is agreement everywhere.
\end{proof}

\centering

\subsection{The dictionary}

Since $|\det B_0|=1$, translation by $B_0\ZZ^2$ is exactly translation of the
Laves tiling by its period lattice, so the cusp fibre is that tiling rolled up
on $\RR^2/B_0\ZZ^2$ (\cref{fig:torus,fig:dp6}):
\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Laves tiling at the Hat point & cusp fibre $W$ & per domain \\
\midrule
hexagon centre (valence 6) & a $dP_6$ component & \SSWComponents \\
edge midpoint (valence 4) & a double curve & \SSWCurves \\
hexagon vertex (valence 3) & a triple point & \SSWPoints \\
kite & a corner of the $dP_6$ hexagon & $6$ \\

Three statements about $W$ proved in~\cite{Alpoge} become statements about the
tiling. First, the rolled-up tiling is a torus:
\begin{equation}
  \chi(\TT)=\underbrace{\bigl(\underbrace{1}_{E_0}+\underbrace{3}_{D}
  +\underbrace{2}_{T}\bigr)}_{\text{6 sites}}
  -\underbrace{\bigl(\underbrace{6}_{\text{centre--edge}}+
  \underbrace{6}_{\text{edge--vertex}}\bigr)}_{\text{12 bonds}}
  +\underbrace{6}_{\text{kites}} = 0 .
  \label{eq:chi}
\end{equation}
Second, the torus orbits of $W$ have Euler characteristic $0$ except the triple
points, so $e(W)=\underbrace{e(E_0^\circ)}_{0}+\underbrace{e(D^\circ)}_{0}
+\underbrace{\#T}_{2}=\SSEW$ counts hexagon vertices in a fundamental domain.
Third, Friedman's triple-point formula~\cite{Friedman}, valid on every double
curve $C$ of $W$ with components $V,V'$ through it, reads
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{(C|_V)^2}_{-1}+\underbrace{(C|_{V'})^2}_{-1}+\underbrace{T_C}_{2}=0 :
\end{equation}
each hexagon side is a $(-1)$-curve and each edge midpoint is bonded to two
hexagon vertices.

\subsection{What descends, and what does not}

\Cref{thm:laves} is an identity of substrates, not of tilings. The Laves tiling
is periodic and its quotient by $B_0\ZZ^2$ is a compact object, the cusp fibre.
A Hat tiling is aperiodic: it is invariant under no translation lattice,
$B_0\ZZ^2$ included, so it does not descend to $W$ or any torus. Both inherit
their lattice from $A_2$, and the six-sphere knows nothing about monotiles; what
they share is that lattice and, by \cref{thm:laves}, the single point of the
monotile family at which the substrate \emph{is} its incidence structure.
\Cref{sec:rollup} tests non-descent on a computed tiling and \cref{sec:mystic}
asks what survives when the tiling is folded onto the torus anyway.

\section{The dual complex of the cusp fibre}
\label{sec:dual}

The dual complex of a normal-crossing fibre has a vertex for each component, an
edge for each double curve and a triangle for each triple point. For $W$ the
incidences are those of the $A_2$ triangulation modulo $\Lam$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:dualcx}
The dual complex $\Delta(W)$ has $1$ vertex $E_0$, $3$ edges $D_a,D_b,D_c$ (along
$e_1$, $e_2-e_1$, $e_2$) and $2$ triangles $P$ (lower) and $Q$ (upper); every
edge borders both triangles. It is the torus, with
\begin{equation}
  \partial P = \underbrace{D_a + D_b - D_c}_{\partial_2 P}, \qquad
  \partial Q = \underbrace{D_c - D_a - D_b}_{\partial_2 Q} = -\partial P,
  \qquad \partial_1 = 0,
  \label{eq:boundary}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
  \begin{aligned}
  &\underbrace{H_0=\ZZ}_{\operatorname{rk}1},\quad
  \underbrace{H_1=\ZZ^3/\langle(1,1,-1)\rangle\cong\ZZ^2}_{\operatorname{rk}2},\quad
  \underbrace{H_2=\ker\partial_2=\ZZ\langle P+Q\rangle}_{\operatorname{rk}1},\\
  &\chi=\overbrace{1-2+1}^{\text{Betti}}=\overbrace{1-3+2}^{V-E+F}=0,
  \end{aligned}
  \label{eq:homology}
\end{equation}
and $\pi_1\Delta(W)=\langle a,b,c\mid ab=c,\ ba=c\rangle=\ZZ^2\cong\Lam$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The vertices of the $A_2$ triangulation form $\Lam$ itself ($|\det B_0|=1$), so
there is one vertex per domain. Its edges fall into three $\Lam$-orbits of
directions, and its triangles into the two orbits ``lower'' $(0,0),(1,0),(0,1)$
and ``upper'' $(1,0),(1,1),(0,1)$ of the unit parallelogram cut along its short
diagonal. Reading the boundary of $P$ round its vertices gives $e_1$, then
$e_2-e_1$, then $-e_2$, which is \eqref{eq:boundary}; the boundary of $Q$ is
the negative. Each edge is a loop, so $\partial_1=0$. The matrix of $\partial_2$
has the single primitive column $(1,1,-1)^T$ up to sign, giving
\eqref{eq:homology}. The fundamental group is generated by the three loops with
one relation per triangle, $ab=c$ from $P$ and $ba=c$ from $Q$, hence $ab=ba$.
The counts and incidences are asserted against \texttt{sixsphere.cusp\_fibre()}
by the figure script before anything is drawn.
\end{proof}

\centering
\includegraphics[width=0.82\textwidth]{ss_dual_complex.pdf}
\caption{The dual complex of $W$. (a) The $A_2$ triangulation modulo
$B_0\ZZ^2$: triangles tinted by class $P$ or $Q$, double curves as orange lines
in three styles (solid $D_a$, dashed $D_b$, dotted $D_c$), the domain as the
black parallelogram. (b) The same complex rolled up: one vertex, three loops,
two triangles.}
\label{fig:dual}
\end{figure}

It has $2E=\underbrace{6}_{\text{oriented edges}}=3F=\underbrace{6}_{\text{(triangle, side)}}$
oriented edges; this number returns in \cref{sec:mystic}.

\section{Rolling up a Hat tiling}
\label{sec:rollup}

\paragraph{The patch.} Tilings are generated by the substitution system of
\texttt{spectre.py}, which builds $\mathrm{Tile}(a,b)$ for any proportions; at
$(1,\sqrt3)$ it is the Hat, with the mystic $\Gamma_2$ at $(\sqrt3,1)$. The
script asserts that every tile vertex lies on a site of the $[3.4.6.4]$ lattice
at $a:b=\sqrt3:1$, every Hat is eight whole kites and every mystic ten. A patch
after $n$ steps is a disc: $443$ tiles for $n=3$ and $656$ for $n=4$.

\paragraph{The certificate.} Call a lattice bond $b$ a \emph{tile boundary},
$\beta(b)=1$, if its two adjacent kites lie in different tiles, and $\beta(b)=0$
otherwise. For a bond orbit $o$ of $\TT$ (six centre--edge and six edge--vertex
orbits) put
\begin{equation}
  f(o)=\frac{\overbrace{\#\{b\in o:\ \beta(b)=1\}}^{\text{boundary bonds in the patch}}}
  {\underbrace{\#\{b\in o\}}_{N_o}} .
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:descent}
If a tiling is invariant under $\Lam$, then $\beta(b+\lambda)=\beta(b)$ for all
$\lambda\in\Lam$, so $\beta$ is constant on each orbit and $f(o)\in\{0,1\}$ for
all twelve orbits.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
A tile boundary is determined by the tiling, and $\lambda$ maps the tiling to
itself, hence maps boundary bonds to boundary bonds and the others to the others.
Orbits of $\TT$ are the $\Lam$-orbits of bonds.
\end{proof}

For the patches, every $f(o)$ lies strictly inside $(0,1)$: from $0.338$ to
$0.470$ for $n=3$ and from $0.323$ to $0.480$ for $n=4$
(\cref{fig:rollup}(b,c)); the script asserts that none is $0$ or $1$. Their mean
is
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\tfrac1{12}\textstyle\sum_o f(o)}_{0.403}\;\approx\;
  \underbrace{\frac{\#\{\text{boundary bonds}\}}{\#\{\text{bonds}\}}}_{0.402}
  \qquad(n=4),
\end{equation}
the two differing only because the orbit sizes $N_o$ differ by a few bonds.

\paragraph{Overlap with translates.} For $t=iE_1+jE_2\ne0$ let
\begin{equation}
  \alpha(t)=\frac{\#\{b:\ b,\,b+t\in\text{patch},\ \beta(b)=\beta(b+t)\}}
  {\underbrace{\#\{b:\ b,\,b+t\in\text{patch}\}}_{>0}} ,
\end{equation}
which would be $1$ for every $t\in\Lam$ if the tiling were invariant. For $n=4$
the best non-identity agreement is $\alpha=0.796$, at $(i,j)=(-3,-5)$
(\cref{fig:overlap}), and no translate gives $1$.

\includegraphics[width=0.4\textwidth]{ss_hat_overlap.pdf}

the statement they illustrate, that a Hat tiling is invariant under no
translation, is the theorem of~\cite{SmithHat} and not something they prove.

\section{Monotile Mystic fibres}
\label{sec:mystic}

\paragraph{Set-up.} Let $E_1=(3,\sqrt3)$, $E_2=(0,2\sqrt3)$, $\Lam=\ZZ E_1+\ZZ E_2$
and $\pi:\RR^2\to\TT=\RR^2/\Lam$. Around each hexagon centre $c\in\Lam$ there are
six kites with vertices $c,\ c+\sqrt3\,u_{30+60k},\ c+2\,u_{60k+60},\
c+\sqrt3\,u_{90+60k}$. Areas fit:
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{|\det(E_1,E_2)|}_{6\sqrt3}=\underbrace{6}_{\text{kites per domain}}
  \times\underbrace{\sqrt3}_{\text{kite}},
\end{equation}
so $\pi$ identifies a kite of the plane with one of the six kites of $\TT$
according to its index $\bmod\,6$. Corner angles are $60^\circ$ (centre),
$90^\circ$ (edge midpoint, twice) and $120^\circ$ (hexagon vertex).

A \emph{mystic} is a copy $gM_0+x$ of a fixed ten-kite polygon $M_0$ with a
distinguished vertex $v_0$ (the first in the vertex list), $g$ a rotation and $x$
a site.

\begin{definition}
Two mystics $M,M'$ of a patch are in the same \emph{fibre class} if $M'=M+\lambda$
for some $\lambda\in\Lam$. The fibre point of $M$ is $\pi$ of its centroid.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:six}
Six kites meet at a site only at a hexagon centre; four meet at an edge midpoint
and three at a hexagon vertex:
$\underbrace{360^\circ/60^\circ}_{6}$, $\underbrace{360^\circ/90^\circ}_{4}$,
$\underbrace{360^\circ/120^\circ}_{3}$. Hence a translation preserving the kite
tiling lies in $\Lam$, and the rotation $\rho$ by $60^\circ$ about a hexagon
centre preserves both the kite tiling and $\Lam$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
A translation preserving the tiling preserves the number of kites at each site,
so it maps centres to centres, which form the single coset $\Lam$ (one per
domain). For $\rho$: $\rho(E_1)=E_2$ and $\rho(E_2)=E_2-E_1$, both in $\Lam$, and
the kite tiling is invariant under the $6$-fold rotation about any centre.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:bound}
In a tiling by whole kites whose mystics are rotations and translations of one
polygon $M_0$ with anchor $v_0$, the mystics fall into at most
$\underbrace{12}_{\text{first-edge angles}}\times\underbrace{6}_{\text{sites mod }\Lam}=72$
fibre classes.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Every tile edge is a union of kite edges, whose directions lie in $30^\circ\ZZ$,
so the first edge of $gM_0$ has at most $12$ angles. The anchor is a site, and
mod $\Lam$ there are $1+3+2=6$ sites. Two mystics with equal angle and equal
anchor mod $\Lam$ differ by an element of $\Lam$.
\end{proof}

\begin{observation}[orientation and anchor]
\label{obs:orient}
For $n=3,4$ the mystics occur in exactly six first-edge angles
$\theta_k=30^\circ+60^\circ k$, $k\in\ZZ/6$ (the $n=2$ patch shows five). In every
patch the anchor $v_0$ is an edge-midpoint site and its class mod $\Lam$ is
determined by the angle:
\[
\begin{array}{c|ccc}
\text{double curve} & D_a & D_c & D_b\\\hline
\text{anchor mod }\Lam & (\tfrac12,0) & (0,\tfrac12) & (\tfrac12,\tfrac12)\\
\theta & 30^\circ,\,210^\circ & 90^\circ,\,270^\circ & 150^\circ,\,330^\circ\\
k & 0,\ 3 & 1,\ 4 & 2,\ 5
\end{array}
\]
So a fibre class is exactly a pair (orientation, anchor), and there are six.
\end{observation}

\paragraph{Why six, structurally.} The six classes are not an arbitrary list.
\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:orbit}
The observed classes are one orbit of the rotation $\rho$ of \cref{lem:six} acting
on $(\theta,\text{anchor})$, and $\rho$ acts freely:
\begin{equation}
  \#\text{orbit}=\frac{\overbrace{|\langle\rho\rangle|}^{6}}
  {\underbrace{|\operatorname{Stab}(M_0)|}_{1}}=6 .
\end{equation}
Under $k\mapsto\rho^k$ the anchor passes through the edge classes in the cyclic
order $D_a\to D_c\to D_b\to D_a\to\cdots$, so by the Chinese remainder theorem
\begin{equation}
  \underbrace{\ZZ/6}_{\text{classes}}\;\cong\;
  \underbrace{\ZZ/3}_{k\bmod3:\ \text{double curve}}\times
  \underbrace{\ZZ/2}_{k\bmod2:\ \text{orientation of the edge}} ,
\end{equation}
that is, (double curve) $\times$ (sign) $=3\times2=2E$.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
$\rho$ preserves the kite tiling and $\Lam$ (\cref{lem:six}), so it maps mystic
placements to mystic placements, raising the first-edge angle by $60^\circ$. The
stabiliser is trivial because $M_0$ has no rotational symmetry (any nontrivial
rotation changes its edge-angle sequence). The edge midpoints of the hexagon at
the origin are $\sqrt3\,u_{30^\circ+60^\circ j}$, which mod $\Lam$ are $E_1/2$,
$E_2/2$ and $(E_2-E_1)/2\equiv(E_1+E_2)/2$ for $j=0,1,2$ and repeat with period
$3$ because $-E_1/2\equiv E_1/2$. These are $D_a,D_c,D_b$. That the observed
classes are exactly this orbit is the content of \cref{obs:orient}.
\end{proof}

\begin{conjecture}
\label{conj:six}
For every $n\ge3$ the mystics of the full level-$n$ patch fall into exactly the
six classes of \cref{obs:orient}, one for each oriented double curve of
$\Delta(W)$.
\end{conjecture}
We have no proof. The data support $\le6$ for $n=3,4$ on a disc, far below the
bound $72$ of \cref{prop:bound}: only $\underbrace{6}_{\text{observed}}/72=1/12$ of
the possible classes occur. A proof would have to come from the metatile
structure of the substitution, since the registration of a tile with the kite
lattice is inherited from the level below.

\paragraph{Same fibre, different surroundings.} Equal fibre points do not
contradict non-descent. For each mystic in the inner $60\%$ of the patch record
the multiset of (label, angle) of the tiles it touches. For $n=3$ three of the six
classes contain two environments among their interior members (the rest one),
and for $n=4$ again three classes show two, with one to nine interior mystics per
class. The counts are small and are an indication only. The logic is the safe
part: a tiling that descends has identical surroundings throughout a class
(\cref{prop:descent}), so a class with two environments is an obstruction, the
tile-level form of the boundary-fraction certificate.

\paragraph{Folding one mystic.} A mystic has ten kites and $\TT$ six, so it covers
$\TT$ $\underbrace{10}_{\text{kites}}/\underbrace{6}_{\text{per domain}}=\tfrac53$
times. The mystic of $30^\circ$ in the three-step patch folds onto the six torus
kites with multiplicities $m=(2,2,1,2,2,1)$, $\sum_k m_k=10$
(\cref{fig:n3}(a)); other orientations give $\rho$-rotations of this pattern.

\paragraph{Open questions.}
\begin{footnotesize}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item Prove or refute \cref{conj:six}; equivalently determine the anchor and
orientation of the mystic in a general metatile.
\item Is the number of neighbourhood types per class bounded, or does it grow with
$n$? This is the quantitative form of non-descent.
\item The same count for the Hat itself (eight kites): the analogue of
\cref{prop:bound} is $72$ (or $144$ with reflections); it has not been computed.
\item Is the identification of the six classes with the oriented double curves of
$\Delta(W)$ natural, for instance through the monodromy of the degeneration, or a
coincidence of the Hat point? We have no argument either way.
\end{enumerate}
\end{footnotesize}

\section{The Flag Class: Block Structure of the Monodromy Matrices}
\label{sec:flag}

Write $V = \ZZ^4$ with basis $(\gamma, u, w, \delta)$. Alpöge's
matrices fix $\gamma$, preserve $\langle \gamma, u, w\rangle$, and act
trivially on the top quotient (\cite[Lemma~2.7]{Alpoge}); so each has the
block form
\begin{equation}
  T = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & r & s \\ 0 & M & c \\ 0 & 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix},
  \qquad M \in \SL_2(\ZZ),\ r \in \ZZ^{1\times 2},\ c \in \ZZ^{2\times 1},
  \label{eq:flag}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is the monodromy of the elliptic curve with period $\tau$ ---
modulo $\gamma$, the first period vector is $\tau u + w$ --- and hence a
Kodaira monodromy \cite{graf2020}. We call pairs $(T_1, T_2)$ of this shape the \emph{flag
class}. Everything in \cref{sec:triangle,sec:forced} is a statement about
the flag class, and about nothing else.

The quantities we need are the paper's. The cusp monodromy is
$T_0 = (T_1T_2)^{-1} = I + N$; the fibre over the cusp is a single copy of
the degree-six del Pezzo surface exactly when $|\det B_0| = 1$, where $B_0$
is induced by $M_0 - I$ on the dual lattice \cite[Lemma~2.6]{Alpoge}; and for twists $\ell_0, \ell_1, \ell_2$ the
presentation of \cite[Theorem~7.17]{Alpoge}, abelianised, gives
\begin{equation}
  |H_1(X)| = |p|, \qquad p = \underbrace{m_1m_2\,\ell_0}_{m_1m_2\ \mid} - \underbrace{m_2\,\ell_1}_{m_2\ \mid} - \underbrace{m_1\,\ell_2}_{m_1\ \mid},
  \label{eq:p}
\end{equation}
with $(m_1, m_2) = (3, 4)$ for the paper. A twist is admissible when the
logarithmic transform it defines is free, which for the paper's data is
$3 \nmid \ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ odd, a rule that follows from a fixed-point computation on the real torus.

\section{Results: Why (3, 4, \texorpdfstring{$\infty$}{infinity}) Is the Only Allowed Triangle Group}
\label{sec:triangle}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:triangle}
In the flag class, a pair $(T_1, T_2)$ of orders $(m_1, m_2)$, with
$(m_1, m_2, \infty)$ hyperbolic, a unipotent cusp, and $|p| = 1$ for some
twist, has $\{m_1, m_2\} = \{3, 4\}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The middle block $M_j$ has the same finite order as $T_j$ (a block-unipotent
matrix of finite order is the identity), and the finite orders in
$\SL_2(\ZZ)$ are $1, 2, 3, 4, 6$. The only element of order two is $-I$,
which is central; if $M_1 = -I$ then $M_0 = (M_1M_2)^{-1} = -M_2^{-1}$ has
trace $-\operatorname{tr} M_2 \in \{1, 0, -1\}$ for $m_2 \in \{3, 4, 6\}$,
so the cusp is not unipotent. This removes every $(2, m)$. Finally, by \eqref{eq:p} each term of $p$ is divisible by $\gcd(m_1,m_2)$, so
\[
  \underbrace{\gcd(m_1,m_2)}_{d}\;\Big|\;p
  \quad\Longrightarrow\quad |p|=1\ \text{requires}\ \underbrace{d=1}_{\text{coprime orders}};
\] among the hyperbolic pairs from
$\{3, 4, 6\}$ only $(3,4)$ is coprime.
\end{proof}

The gcd alone therefore disposes of $(3,3)$, $(4,4)$ and $(6,6)$. The
survey finds that they fail earlier too, for a reason of independent
interest:

\begin{sloppypar}
The congruence is short enough to machine-check for every twist: the Lean
facts \texttt{gcd\_obstruction\_3\_6} and \texttt{gcd\_obstruction\_4\_6}
state $3 \mid 18\ell_0 - 6\ell_1 - 3\ell_2$ and $2 \mid 24\ell_0 - 6\ell_1 -
4\ell_2$ for all integers, by \texttt{omega}.
\end{sloppypar}

\begin{observation}
\label{obs:sign}
Enumerating $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ elements in a box, $(4,4)$ has no pair whose
product is $\pm$(unipotent), and $(3,3)$ and $(6,6)$ have parabolic
products only as $-$(unipotent): an $\mathrm{I}_2^*$ cusp, never
$\mathrm{I}_n$. The outcome is the same in the boxes $\pm 4$ and $\pm 7$.
For $(3,3)$ the mechanism is visible in $\mathrm{PSL}_2(\ZZ)
= \langle s\rangle * \langle t\rangle$, where $t \cdot sts^{-1} = (ts)^2$ is
parabolic, but its lifts to $\SL_2(\ZZ)$ carry the wrong sign. We have not
turned this into a proof.
\end{observation}

\section{Uniqueness of the Lattice Constraints: the Alpöge Matrices}
\label{sec:forced}

\subsection{The families}

Conjugating \eqref{eq:flag} by $U = \bigl(\begin{smallmatrix} 1 & x & z \\
0 & I & y \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)$ fixes $M$ and acts by
\begin{equation}
  r \mapsto r + x(M - I), \qquad c \mapsto c + (I - M)\,y,
  \label{eq:action}
\end{equation}
with the \emph{same} $x$ and $y$ on both generators (derived symbolically).
Since $M_2 - I$ is invertible over $\QQ$, with
$\det(M_2-I)=\underbrace{\det M_2}_{1}-\underbrace{\operatorname{tr}M_2}_{0}+1=2$
for the order-four block, the data of $T_2$ reduces to $2 \times 2$ residue classes --- and that
spends all of $x$ and $y$. Nothing is left to normalise $T_1$: its $r$ and
$c$ are genuinely free, and the extensions come in infinite families.
Given $r$ and $c$, the corner $s$ is fixed by $T^m = I$, whose corner reads
$ms + f(r,c) = 0$ and need not have an integer solution.

\subsection{The classes}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:classes}
The \SSUnimod{} survivors form \SSClassesWord{} $\GL_4(\ZZ)$-conjugacy
classes of \SSClassSize{}. Exactly one class can reach $|p| = 1$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Membership: for each survivor, the conjugating matrix is found by solving
the linear system $P T_j = T'_j P$ exactly and searching its integer points;
each is verified. Distinctness: the set of values of $|p|$, taken modulo
$m_1m_2 = 12$ as $\min(x, 12 - x)$, that admissible twists can reach is a
conjugacy invariant --- the functional $\gamma$ that reads off $\ell_j$ is
canonical up to sign --- and it takes a different value on each class:
$\SSGoodInvariant$ on one, and \SSOtherInvariants{} on the others. Only the
first contains $1$.
\end{proof}

The proof of distinctness does not depend on the conjugator search, which
could in principle miss a large conjugator: two classes with different
invariants cannot be conjugate, however hard one looks (\cref{fig:classes}).

This is checked by multiplication, and in Lean (\texttt{paper\_conjugator},
by \texttt{decide}). The other three classes bottom out at $|p| =
\SSNearMissMins$ respectively. Whether any of them supports the period
functions of the construction --- and so a compact complex threefold at all,
a rational homology sphere in the cases $|p| = 2, 3$ --- is a question about
the analytic side that we do not address.

\section{Orientation Reversal and Lattice Duality}
\label{sec:mirror}

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:achiral}
Reversing the orientation of the base replaces $(T_1, T_2)$ by
$(T_1^{-1}, T_2^{-1})$ \cite[Remark~3.18]{Alpoge}. The two pairs are
conjugate:
\[
  P = \SSMirrorConj, \qquad \det P = \SSMirrorDet,
\]
satisfies $P T_j P^{-1} = T_j^{-1}$ for $j = 1, 2$.
\end{proposition}

The monodromy therefore cannot distinguish $X$ from its orientation-reversed
counterpart. This is a statement about the lattice, not a proof that $X$ is
biholomorphic to its conjugate. It is machine-checked (\texttt{lattice\_achiral}). The involution is orientation reversal of the base; it preserves the reachable $|p|$ residues $\{1,5\}$ (as it must, since $|p|$ is insensitive to sign), and the lattice data is a fixed point, in contrast with the Spectre, which is strictly chiral~\cite{SmithSpectre}.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:dual}
The monodromy lattice $V$ and its dual are isomorphic over $\QQ$ but not
over $\ZZ$: every integral intertwiner has determinant $\SSDualDetForm$ in
the coordinates $(s, t)$ of the rank-two lattice of intertwiners.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The invariant alternating form $Q_0$ of \cite[Lemma~2.8]{Alpoge} satisfies
$Q_0 T = T^{-t} Q_0$, so it intertwines $V$ and its dual over $\QQ$; but
$\det Q_0 = \SSDetQZero$. The intertwiners form a rank-two lattice, and the
determinant of the general one factors as $\SSDualDetForm$, which is never
$\pm 1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{sloppypar}
Alpöge shows the construction carries no polarization, because the form
$b(x, y) = Q_0(x, Ny)$ is indefinite. \Cref{prop:dual} is a sharper,
integral statement: there is not even a non-positive \emph{principal} one.
\end{sloppypar}

\section{Arithmetic Invariants and Resemblances to the Spectre}
\label{sec:arith}

The special values of the construction's period functions lie at the
elliptic points, $\tau(z_1) = e^{i\pi/3}$ and $\tau(z_2) = i$, generating
$\QQ(\sqrt{-3}, i) = \QQ(\zeta_{12})$. Its quadratic square classes are
$\SSClassesPeriods$ --- signed: dropping the sign would report the classes of the real field $\QQ(\sqrt3)$
and manufacture an identity with the monotile's geometry. With signs kept:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
field & square classes & shared with $\QQ(\zeta_{12})$ \\
\midrule
six-sphere periods, $\QQ(\zeta_{12})$ & $\SSClassesPeriods$ & --- \\
monotile geometry, $\QQ(\sqrt3)$ & $\{1, 3\}$ & $\SSShareGeometry$ \\
Hat inflation, $\QQ(\sqrt5)$ & $\{1, 5\}$ & $\{1\}$ \\
Spectre inflation, $\QQ(\sqrt6, \sqrt{10})$ & $\SSClassesSpectre$ & $\{1\}$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

The six-sphere shares the monotiles' \emph{geometry} field and none of their
\emph{inflation} fields --- consistent with \cref{sec:laves}, where it shares
the substrate and not the tiling.

Two further facts sharpen the picture. The Euclidean triangle groups are
$(2,3,6)$, $(2,4,4)$ and $(3,3,3)$; none contains both a $3$ and a $4$, so
the base orbifold of the construction is necessarily hyperbolic, while the
substrate's symmetry is the Euclidean $(2,3,6)$. And the cusp component's
polygon is the hexagon of the toric surface $B_3$, whose hopping vectors are
those of the triangular lattice: the mirror curve of the component is the
triangular-lattice Hofstadter model of~\cite{chen2025,shaffer2022}.

\paragraph{The $\sqrt6$.} The form $b = 6w^2 - \delta^2$ has isotropic
slopes $\pm\sqrt6$, and the Spectre's inflation factor is $\lambda =
(\sqrt6 + \sqrt{10})/2$, with $\lambda^2 = 4 + \sqrt{15}$: the two share the
square class $\SSShareLatticeSpectre$. The origins are unrelated. The
six-sphere's $6$ is $Q_0(u,w)$, forced by the entry $-6$ of $T_1$, which the period laws pin down; the Spectre's is a consequence of
its substitution matrix. Nothing downstream of either uses the other.

\paragraph{Valence four.} The Laves valences $(6, 4, 3)$ echo the orbifold
orders $(3, 4, \infty)$. But the valence-four sites are the double curves of
$W$, which lie over the cusp $p_0$ --- not over $p_2$, the point of order
four, whose fibre is a multiple fibre with no double curves at all.

\section{An Integer Inflation on the Same Kind of Torus: Chair44}
\label{sec:chair44}

\Cref{sec:rollup} ends with a negative result: no Hat tiling descends to $\TT$,
and a four-step Hat patch agrees with no non-trivial lattice translate of itself
beyond $0.796$ (\cref{fig:overlap}). That is what an aperiodic tiling does when
its inflation factor is irrational. Tsiokos's \emph{Chair44}~\cite{Tsiokos1}
is an aperiodic tile of the other kind. It is a seven-cube chair, the
$2\times2\times2$ cube minus a corner, decorated so that it is claimed to tile only
aperiodically, and its substitution has eight children and inflation factor the
\emph{integer} $2$. That the decorated tile is aperiodic, and an einstein tile, is
Tsiokos's claimed proof, which we quote; whether every tiling lies in the substitution
hull is open upstream. What we compute is a finite and exact comparison, in \texttt{pyCICY.chair44\_nested}, which carries its own copy of the
eight child poses and re-checks that they tile each of the $24$ rotated doubled
chairs. The six-sphere contains no Chair44; the section sets two kinds of tiling
side by side on tori.

\subsection{Why nested tori}
\label{sec:chair-nested}

An integer inflation is a lattice map. A level-$M$ supertile is a Chair44 scaled by
$2^M$, so level-$M$ supertiles are placed on the sublattice $2^M\ZZ^3\subset\ZZ^3$
of index $8^M$, and a level-$M$ block of unit cells has $8^M$ cells. The natural
family of tori is $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$, each covering the last. The Hat's and the
Spectre's inflations are irrational (\cref{sec:arith}), so they leave no such
family.

This echoes, as a count and nothing more, Remark~4.9 of~\cite{Alpoge}, as reproduced
for the cusp fibre in \cref{sec:laves}: a sublattice of index $d$ of the cusp lattice is
$d$ copies of the fundamental domain, giving $d$ components and $e(W)=2d$. In the plane
the nested sublattices would have index $4^M$; for Chair44 they have index $8^M$. The cusp
fibre is two-dimensional, no complex fourfold with a Chair44 cusp fibre is constructed
here, and none is claimed.

\subsection{Coincidence, and its rate}
\label{sec:chair-rate}

The substitution acts on the alphabet $\mathcal A$ of $\SSChLabels{}=24\times7$
labelled unit cells (a rotation and a cell of the chair): $\sigma$ sends a label to the
eight labels of its $2\times2\times2$ block, every column sum of $\sigma$ is $8$, and
$\sigma$ is primitive. For $a\in\{0,\dots,2^M-1\}^3$ let $\sigma^M(\ell)(a)$ be the label
of the cell at address $a$ inside the block of $\ell$ after $M$ steps. The address $a$
is \emph{coincident} at depth $M$ if $\sigma^M(\ell)(a)$ does not depend on $\ell$.
Let $c(M)$ be the number of addresses that are not, and
$E(M)=\sum_a\bigl(\lvert\{\sigma^M(\ell)(a):\ell\in\mathcal A\}\rvert-1\bigr)$. The least
depth with a coincident address is $\SSChCoincDepth{}$, and then $\SSChCoincAddr{}$ of
the $\SSChCoincTotal{}$ addresses are coincident, as recorded upstream
(\cite{Tsiokos1}, the modular coincidence).

\begin{proposition}[the rate]
\label{prop:chair-rate}
For every $M\ge1$,
\[
c(M)=\SSChCCoef\cdot2^M-\SSChCConst,\qquad E(M)=\SSChECoef\cdot2^M+\SSChEConst .
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrrr}
\toprule
$M$ & 3 & 4 & 5 & 6 & 7 & 8 \\
\midrule
addresses $8^M$ & \SSChRowAddr \\
$c(M)$ & \SSChRowC \\
$E(M)$ & \SSChRowE \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\begin{proof}
Reading the digits of an address from the most significant, the set of labels still
possible is obtained from $\mathcal A$ by applying $M$ of the eight maps
$A\mapsto\{\sigma(\ell)_b:\ell\in A\}$. The reachable sets form an automaton with
$\SSChSets{}$ states, $\SSChNonSingleton{}$ of which have more than one element. A
singleton goes to a singleton, so $c(M)$ and $E(M)$ are linear functionals of $T^Me_0$,
with $T$ the transfer matrix on the non-singleton states. By Cayley--Hamilton each
satisfies a linear recurrence of order at most $\SSChNonSingleton{}$; the closed form
satisfies one of order two, with roots $2$ and $1$. Their difference satisfies a
recurrence of order at most $\SSChNonSingleton{}+2=\SSChTermsNeeded{}$, and vanishes for
all $M\ge1$ as soon as it vanishes for $\SSChTermsNeeded{}$ consecutive values. It
vanishes for $M=1,\dots,\SSChTermsChecked{}$, checked in exact integer arithmetic.
\end{proof}

The closed form for $c$ holds from $M=1$ and fails at $M=0$. The fraction of undetermined
addresses, $c(M)/8^M\sim28\cdot4^{-M}$, tends to zero.

\subsection{How nearly the tiling descends}
\label{sec:chair-descent}

\begin{proposition}[descent to the nested tori]
\label{prop:chair-descent}
Let $P$ be a finite patch that is a union of whole level-$M$ blocks, the block at
$2^My$, $y\in Y$, carrying the labels $\sigma^M(\ell_y)$. For $v\in\ZZ^3$, among the cells
$x\in P$ with $x+2^Mv\in P$, the labels at $x$ and $x+2^Mv$ differ for at most a fraction
$c(M)/8^M$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Such cells are $x=2^My+a$ with $y,y+v\in Y$ and $a$ an address. The labels at $x$ and
$x+2^Mv$ are $\sigma^M(\ell_y)(a)$ and $\sigma^M(\ell_{y+v})(a)$, which agree whenever $a$
is coincident, whatever the two parent labels are. At most $c(M)$ of the $8^M$ addresses
are not.
\end{proof}

The proposition has no experiment in it. The patch $P_N$ obtained by applying $\sigma^N$
to the seven cells of one chair is a union of whole level-$M$ blocks for every $M\le N$.
For $N=6$ it has $\SSChPatchCells{}$ cells, and the inequality holds on all
$\SSChAgreeRows{}$ translates tested, three axes and $M=1,\dots,5$. Along $x$:

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{rcc}
\toprule
translate $2^M$ & agreement & proved lower bound $1-c(M)/8^M$ \\
\midrule
$2$ $(M=1)$ & $0$ & $0$ \\
$4$ $(M=2)$ & $\SSChAgreeTwo$ & $\SSChBoundTwo$ \\
$8$ $(M=3)$ & $\SSChAgreeThree$ & $\SSChBoundThree$ \\
$16$ $(M=4)$ & $\SSChAgreeFour$ & $\SSChBoundFour$ \\
$32$ $(M=5)$ & $\SSChAgreeFive$ & $\SSChBoundFive$ \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

Translation by $2$ agrees on no cell, and neither does any odd translate tested; the
disagreement then falls by about a factor of four per step, the same $4^{-M}$ as the
bound. The Hat's best non-identity value, $0.796$, was measured on the agreement of the
\emph{boundary pattern} of a four-step patch with translates of the Laves lattice
(\cref{sec:rollup}), a different quantity on a different footing, so the comparison
is a contrast of kind and not a measured gap.

\begin{observation}[near-periods]
\label{obs:chair-periods}
On the level-$5$ patch, with $\SSChScanCells{}$ cells, the agreement with \emph{every}
translate whose overlap is at least an eighth of the patch (the autocorrelation of the
$\SSChLabels{}$ label indicators, by FFT, in integers) is at least $0.5$ for
$\SSChScanHalf{}$ translates, at least $0.9$ for $\SSChScanNinety{}$ and at least $0.99$
for $\SSChScanFull{}$. Writing each count by the $2$-adic valuation of the translation
vector: $0.5$: \SSChScanHalfVal{}; $0.9$: \SSChScanNinetyVal{}; $0.99$: \SSChScanFullVal{}.
\end{observation}

Every near-period is divisible by a power of $2$, none has valuation $0$ or $1$ at the
$0.9$ level, and the six at $0.99$ are the corner translates $(\pm32,\pm32,\pm32)$, as
large as the patch allows. This is one patch; \cref{prop:chair-descent} is what is proved.

\subsection{What remains undetermined}
\label{sec:chair-undet}

The transfer matrix of \cref{prop:chair-rate} on the $\SSChNonSingleton{}$ non-singleton
sets has characteristic polynomial $\SSChCharPoly$, and spectral radius exactly $2$.
Since $c(M)\sim2^M$ inside $8^M$ addresses, the set of addresses where the label is not
yet determined has box dimension $\log_22=1$ in the $2$-adic internal space $\ZZ_2^3$, in
keeping with window boundaries of measure zero.

\begin{observation}[six candidate rotations]
\label{obs:chair-six}
At $M=\SSChUndetM$, of the $8^{\SSChUndetM}$ addresses $\SSChUndet{}$ are undetermined.
Of these, $\SSChSix{}$ carry exactly six candidate labels, whose six rotations are in
every case a coset of an order-six subgroup of the rotation group of the cube (one
identity, three half-turns, two third-turns, all fixing a body diagonal as an axis), and
$\SSChTwentyFour{}$ carry $24$ labels.
\end{observation}

The substitution thus fixes the orientation of such a cell up to the dihedral group of a
body diagonal, which contains the symmetry the undecorated chair has about its own
diagonal. The number six is not related here to the six mystic fibre classes of
\cref{sec:mystic}, where it counts the oriented edges of the dual complex; no mechanism is
known, and we record it as a coincidence.

\subsection{Two matches that are not connections}
\label{sec:chair-nolink}

\paragraph{A three-dimensional cusp analogue.} The $A_2$ triangulation behind the cusp
fan is Kuhn's triangulation of $\ZZ^2$. Its three-dimensional version, Kuhn's
triangulation of $\ZZ^3$, has $1,7,12,6$ simplices per fundamental domain ($\chi=0$; the
plane has $1,3,2$) and $24=4!$ simplices round a vertex, whose dual polytope, the
truncated octahedron, plays the role of the hexagon of the $dP_6$. The count $24$ is
also the order of the rotation group of the cube, the orientation group of Chair44, and
one would like it to be a symmetry. It is not: of the $\SSChKuhnGroup{}$ elements of the
cube group, $\SSChKuhnPreserve{}$ preserve the complex ($\SSChKuhnRot{}$ of the $24$
rotations); the complex has a linear automorphism group of order $\SSChKuhnAut{}$ meeting
the cube group in those $\SSChKuhnMeet{}$ elements; and the two groups are not conjugate
in $\GL(3,\ZZ)$, because reduced modulo two the cube group fixes a nonzero vector of
$\mathbb F_2^3$ and the other fixes none. The same mismatch is visible in the plane, where
the cusp torus is hexagonal and the square lattice is not. So $24=24$ is numerology, and
Chair44 does not sit equivariantly on this complex.

\paragraph{The orbifold group.} The rotation group of the cube is a quotient of the base
orbifold group $\mathbb Z/3*\mathbb Z/4$ of the construction, with the cusp loop sent to
a half-turn (the $(2,3,4)$ triangle group). The corresponding $S_4$ cover of the orbifold
is smooth, of genus $\SSChCoverGenus{}$ with $\SSChCoverCusps{}$ cusps
(Riemann--Hurwitz). This is exact group theory; nothing computed here shows that the
monodromy representation of the six-sphere factors through the quotient.

\subsection{What this says}

The Hat's substrate descends to $\TT$ and the Hat does not. Chair44's inflation is a
lattice map, so it has a whole tower of tori to nearly descend to, with a defect that is
exactly $c(M)/8^M\sim28\cdot4^{-M}$ (\cref{prop:chair-descent}). That is the finite
shadow of what the cited theory states in the limit: a primitive lattice substitution
with a modular coincidence has pure point, limit-periodic structure
\cite{LeeMoody,Schlottmann,LMS}, which we quote and do not re-prove. All
$\SSChChecks{}$ checks of \texttt{chair44\_nested.status} pass. Nothing here shows that
every Chair44 tiling lies in the substitution hull.

\section{Central Charges on Calabi--Yau Threefolds}
\label{sec:links}

We ask what the six-sphere's finite layer shares with three standard objects: a
reflexive polygon, the lattice $H^2(K3,\ZZ)$ with Kodaira's classification, and
Bridgeland central charges on Calabi--Yau threefolds. Each number below is
reached by at least two routes.

\subsection{The cusp component and the volume product}
\label{sec:links-mahler}

The component of the cusp fibre is the degree-six del Pezzo surface $dP_6$,
the toric surface whose polygon is $B_3$, the hexagon with \SSDPBoundary{}
boundary points. Its anticanonical degree $K^2$ is the normalised volume
$V(B_3) = 2\,\mathrm{Area}(B_3)$ (the identity $V(P) = (-K)^n$ for the
Gorenstein toric Fano variety whose anticanonical polytope is the reflexive
polytope $P$; $B_3$ is self-dual, so there is no ambiguity about which of the
pair is meant).

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:dp6}
$K^2_{dP_6} = \SSDPKTwo$. This is computed four ways, with no shared code:
by triangulating the hexagon ($V(B_3) = \SSDPVolume$); by Ehrhart counts of
the same polygon; by Noether's formula $K^2 = 12 - n = 12 - \SSDPRays$ for a
smooth complete toric surface with $n$ rays~\cite{Fulton}; and as $9 - 3$ for
$\mathbb{P}^2$ blown up at three points. The toric divisors have self-intersections $(-1)^{\times 6}$, and
\[
  \underbrace{\textstyle\sum_{i=1}^{n} D_i^2}_{(-1)\times 6\,=\,-6}
  =\underbrace{12-3n}_{n=6}
  \qquad\text{while}\qquad
  K^2=\underbrace{12-n}_{n=6}=6 ,
\]
as they must.
\end{proposition}

The hexagon is self-dual: $V(B_3^*) = \SSDPDual$, so the volume product is
$V(B_3)V(B_3^*) = \SSDPProduct$. It is centrally symmetric, and the Mahler
bound for symmetric bodies is $4^2\cdot 2! = \SSDPBoundSym$, so $B_3$ lies
strictly above it (only two reflexive polygons attain it). The number that
the dictionary of \cref{sec:laves} reads off as six kites per domain, six
$(-1)$-curves of the hexagon, six corners, is therefore also the volume of
the polytope; the agreement is the toric fact that a smooth toric surface has
as many torus-fixed points as rays, together with $K^2 = 12 - n$, and it is
specific to $n = 6$. We claim nothing beyond that.

\subsection{The elliptic fibration and the lattice module}
\label{sec:links-lattice}

The middle block of the monodromy is that of an elliptic family over
$\mathbb{P}^1$ with singular fibres over the three points of the orbifold,
so each local monodromy fixes a Kodaira type up to the usual inversion: order
$3$ means $\mathrm{IV}$ or $\mathrm{IV}^*$, order $4$ means $\mathrm{III}$ or
$\mathrm{III}^*$, and the cusp, being unipotent, is of type
$\mathrm{I}_n$ ($n = 1$ here). The Euler numbers of the singular fibres of an elliptic
surface over $\mathbb{P}^1$ sum to $12\,\chi(\mathcal{O})$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:kodaira}
For orders $(3,4)$ and an $\mathrm{I}_1$ cusp there are \SSKodairaCandidates{}
candidate triples, with Euler totals $\SSKodairaTotals$. Exactly one is a
multiple of $12$:
\[
  \SSKodairaTriple, \qquad
  \underbrace{8}_{e(\mathrm{IV}^*)} + \underbrace{3}_{e(\mathrm{III})}
  + \underbrace{1}_{e(\mathrm{I}_1)} = \underbrace{12}_{12\chi(\mathcal O)} .
\]
Its Jacobian is a rational elliptic surface; a K3 (total $24$) is impossible.
\end{proposition}

The same enumeration reproduces the two examples of \cref{sec:triangle},
$\mathrm{IV}^*+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{I}_2$ for $(3,6)$ and
$\mathrm{III}^*+\mathrm{II}+\mathrm{I}_1$ for $(4,6)$, and so is checked
against the paper's own Euler arithmetic before being used for $(3,4)$. The
trivial lattice of such a surface is $U \oplus R$ with $R$ the root lattice
of the reducible fibres, and the Mordell--Weil rank is
$10 - 2 - \operatorname{rk}R$.

\begin{proposition}
\label{prop:roots}
The root lattices $E_6 \oplus A_1$ and $E_7$ embed in $E_8$ both integrally
and over $\QQ$. Integrally: the $\SSERootsEight$ roots of $E_8$ are generated
from its Cartan matrix; the roots orthogonal to one root $\alpha$ form $E_7$
($\SSERootsSeven$ roots, rank $7$, discriminant \SSEDiscSeven{}); those
orthogonal to an $A_2 \ni \alpha$ form $E_6$ ($\SSERootsSix$ roots, rank
$6$, discriminant \SSEDiscSix{}), and $A_1 = \langle \alpha \rangle \subset
A_2$ is orthogonal to it, giving $E_6 \oplus A_1$ of discriminant
\SSEDiscSixA{}. Over $\QQ$ the Hasse--Minkowski test of
\texttt{pyCICY.k3lattice} accepts both. A control fails as it should:
$E_7 \oplus \langle 3 \rangle$ has the right rank but the wrong
discriminant and does not embed. In all three configurations ($\mathrm{IV}^*
+ \mathrm{III} + \mathrm{I}_1$, $\mathrm{IV}^* + \mathrm{II} + \mathrm{I}_2$,
$\mathrm{III}^* + \mathrm{II} + \mathrm{I}_1$) the Mordell--Weil rank is
$\SSMWRank$.
\end{proposition}

So the paper's $(3,4,\infty)$ data and its $(3,6,\infty)$ example have the
same root part, $E_6 \oplus A_1$, although the fibres differ. The scope is
narrow. Euler counting shows that the local data is consistent with a
rational elliptic surface of that configuration; it does not show that the
six-sphere's family \emph{is} such a surface, and whether the configuration
exists is a question about the Miranda--Persson classification~\cite{MP86}
that we do not decide. The $\QQ$-embedding is a necessary condition only;
the integral statement is the explicit construction inside $E_8$.

\subsection{Where the central-charge module stops}
\label{sec:links-cc}

The central-charge module computes the charges $Z(E) = -\int e^{-B-i\omega}
\mathrm{ch}(E)$ (ordinary and square-root-Todd) of the large-volume
stability conditions announced for smooth projective threefolds with trivial
canonical bundle. They need a real class $\omega$ with $\omega^3 > 0$. The
six-sphere has $H^2(X,\mathbb{R}) = 0$, so it carries no such class: it is
not K\"ahler, and the module's own guard \SSGuardRefused{} the empty class. We record this as a result rather than a gap. The
finite layer behind the six-sphere is rigid, but the charges that give the
CICY side its stability-theoretic content have no counterpart here.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

The cusp fibre of Alpöge's six-sphere is the aperiodic monotile's substrate, at
the monotile's own distinguished point, rolled up on a torus
(\cref{thm:laves}). Folding a computed Hat tiling onto that torus, the tiling does
not descend, as it cannot: every one of the twelve bond orbits is sometimes a tile
boundary and sometimes not (\cref{prop:descent}, \cref{sec:rollup}). The mystic
tile, however, lands on exactly six points of the torus in the patches computed,
one per orientation, each anchored over a double curve of $W$ and forming a single
orbit of the hexagon's rotation group (\cref{prop:orbit}); six is the number of
oriented edges of the dual complex $\Delta(W)$ (\cref{prop:dualcx}). Proved is the
bound of $72$ classes; the count of six is a conjecture backed by patches of three
and four substitution steps, and the dependence of surroundings on class rests on
small samples.

The Hat does not descend to $\TT$ because its inflation is irrational. Chair44, whose
inflation is the integer $2$, nearly descends to every torus $\RR^3/2^M\ZZ^3$ of a nested
tower, with a defect bounded by $c(M)/8^M\sim28\cdot4^{-M}$ that is proved from a finite
recurrence certificate and measured on a patch of $\SSChPatchCells$ cells
(\cref{prop:chair-rate,prop:chair-descent}). That is a contrast of kind. The six-sphere
contains no Chair44, and two matches with it, the $24$ simplices round a vertex of the
Kuhn triangulation of $\ZZ^3$ and the $(3,4,\infty)$ group against the cube's rotation
group, are coincidences (\cref{sec:chair-nolink}).

The lattice data was forced: within the flag class the triangle group by proof, the
matrices up to conjugacy in every range searched, with finiteness of the search
space the one step left unproved. The lattice is achiral and not self-dual over
$\ZZ$, its arithmetic meets the monotiles' in their geometry and not in their
inflation, and two resemblances with the Spectre are coincidences.

None of this bears on whether the construction is correct; that rests on analysis
and topology and on the external formalization~\cite{Alexeev}. The six-sphere
knows nothing of monotiles, and we draw no physical conclusion: the substrate
identity, the six fibres and the contacts of \cref{sec:links} are identities of
integers and of finite patches.

\subsection*{Limitations} 
Everything in \cref{sec:triangle,sec:forced} is
restricted to the flag class; lattice data outside it is not searched.
\Cref{obs:sign,obs:stable} are finite searches. \Cref{thm:laves} is a
statement about substrates. The fibre counts of \cref{sec:rollup,sec:mystic} are
computed on finite substitution patches (the four-step patch is a radius-cut
disc); \cref{conj:six} is a conjecture, and the neighbourhood counts of
\cref{sec:mystic} rest on one to nine interior mystics per class. \Cref{sec:links}: the Kodaira
configuration and the lattice embeddings are consistency statements for the
local monodromy, not statements that the six-sphere's fibration is that
elliptic surface, and the rational embedding is a necessary condition only.
Every analytic question --- whether the
near-miss classes support a complex structure, whether $X$ is biholomorphic
to its conjugate --- is declined, as is the diffeomorphism $X \cong S^6$
itself. \Cref{sec:chair44}: \cref{prop:chair-rate,prop:chair-descent} are proved; \cref{obs:chair-periods}
is one patch and \cref{obs:chair-six} is a finite automaton at $M=\SSChUndetM$. Chair44's
aperiodicity and einstein property are Tsiokos's claimed proof, the pure point
and limit-periodic structure is a cited theorem
\cite{LeeMoody,Schlottmann,LMS}, and whether every tiling lies in the substitution hull
is open upstream; none is re-proved here. The comparison with the Hat's $0.796$ is on a
different footing, and no complex fourfold with a Chair44 cusp fibre is constructed.
